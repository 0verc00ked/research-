# US Private Equity in Healthcare — Deep Evidence Dossier (International Comparator Cell)

**Investigation:** Foreign Private Equity and Institutional Capital in India's Healthcare, Pharmaceutical, Food and Agriculture Systems
**Cell:** International — US PE in healthcare (deep, with numbers)
**Compiled:** 2026-06-24
**Purpose:** This is the US comparator evidence base. It documents what has happened to patient outcomes, prices, staffing, market concentration, and corporate finance when US private equity (PE), sovereign-wealth, pension, and institutional capital acquired healthcare assets. It is intended to inform the analysis of analogous foreign-capital dynamics in Indian healthcare. All figures carry an evidence classification and source URL.

**Evidence classification key:**
- **VERIFIED FACT** — stated in a regulator filing/order, court document, government report, company release, or peer-reviewed study's reported result.
- **STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION** — well-supported by one or more rigorous studies/official sources; widely corroborated.
- **EXPERT INTERPRETATION** — analyst, academic, or advocacy synthesis of facts.
- **PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS** — reasonable inference, not directly proven.
- **UNPROVEN CLAIM** — asserted (often in litigation/press) but not adjudicated/verified.

**Currency note:** US healthcare figures are native USD. Where useful for the India-facing investigation, USD is converted to INR at an indicative ~₹83/USD (2024) or ~₹86/USD (2026); conversions are approximate and flagged.

## 1. Overview

Across the strongest available evidence — peer-reviewed studies in JAMA, NEJM-adjacent journals, Health Affairs, Annals of Internal Medicine, and NBER, plus FTC/DOJ/HHS, Senate, and GAO documents — a consistent pattern emerges in US PE-acquired healthcare:

1. **Worse safety outcomes in acute settings.** PE acquisition of hospitals was associated with a **25.4% increase in hospital-acquired conditions** (Kannan/Bruch/Song, JAMA 2023), including a ~**38% rise in central-line infections** and ~**27% rise in falls**; PE acquisition of nursing homes was associated with a **~10–11% increase in short-term mortality** (Gupta/Howell/Yannelis, NBER/Review of Financial Studies). [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2813379) | [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28474)
2. **Staffing and cost extraction as the mechanism.** PE hospitals cut full-time staff ~**11.6%** and salary spend ~**16.6%**; PE nursing homes saw lease payments **+75%** and interest **+~325%** (Annals 2025; Gupta et al.). [Annals](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/ANNALS-24-03471)
3. **Higher prices via roll-ups and concentration.** Single PE firms exceed **30% market share in 108 metro specialty markets** and **50% in 50 markets**; PE hospitals raised prices **7–16%**, PE physician practices **4–20%** (HHS/FTC/DOJ 2025; Bruch et al. Health Affairs 2023). [Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2023.00152) | [HHS report](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/hhs-consolidation-health-care-markets-rfi-response-report.pdf)
4. **Financial fragility and collapse.** Surprise-billing arbitrage models (Envision/KKR; TeamHealth/Blackstone) and sale-leaseback debt structures (Steward/Cerberus + Medical Properties Trust) produced major bankruptcies in 2023–2024. Nearly **90% of the most financially distressed healthcare companies are PE-owned** (PESP). [PESP](https://pestakeholder.org/news/almost-90-of-most-distressed-healthcare-companies-are-owned-by-pe-firms/)
5. **A regulatory turn.** FTC sued Welsh Carson/USAP (2023) over an anesthesia roll-up; FTC/DOJ/HHS launched a cross-government RFI (March 2024) and published a critical report (Jan 2025); 7+ states enacted PE-oversight laws in 2025.

**Mechanisms repeatedly identified:** leverage/debt loading, sale-leaseback of real estate (rent extraction), serial "roll-up" add-on acquisitions below antitrust reporting thresholds, staffing cuts, dividend recapitalizations/management fees, and billing strategies (out-of-network surprise billing, upcoding, service-line cherry-picking).

## 2. Nursing Homes — the Gupta/Howell/Yannelis mortality evidence

**Core study:** Atul Gupta, Sabrina T. Howell, Constantine Yannelis, Abhinav Gupta, "Owner Incentives and Performance in Healthcare: Private Equity Investment in Nursing Homes," NBER Working Paper 28474 (released **Feb 13, 2021**); published in *The Review of Financial Studies* 37(4):1029–1077 (2024).
Sources: [NBER w28474](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28474) | [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/202104/how-patients-fare-when-private-equity-funds-acquire-nursing-homes) | [RFS/Oxford](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/37/4/1029/7441509) | [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3537612)

| Finding | Figure | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Sample | >7 million Medicare patients, 2005–2017 | VERIFIED FACT |
| Short-term mortality increase (PE vs. control) | **~10%** (instrumented local average treatment effect **~11%**) | VERIFIED FACT (study result) |
| Implied excess deaths, 2005–2017 | **20,150 Medicare lives** | VERIFIED FACT (study estimate) |
| Mortality cost | **~$21 billion** (2016 dollars) | VERIFIED FACT (study estimate) |
| Taxpayer spending per short-term stay | **+11%** (overall patient bills >10% higher) | VERIFIED FACT |
| Nursing-assistant hours | **−3%** (frontline caregivers); overall staffing **−1.4%** | VERIFIED FACT |
| Antipsychotic medication probability | **+50%** | VERIFIED FACT |
| Management fees post-buyout | **+7.7%** | VERIFIED FACT |
| Lease payments post-buyout | **+75%** | VERIFIED FACT |
| Interest payments post-buyout | **+~325%** | VERIFIED FACT |
| Cash on hand | **−~38%** | VERIFIED FACT |
| PE share of skilled nursing facilities | <1% (2005) → ~9% (2015) | VERIFIED FACT |

**Mechanism (study's own framing):** declines in patient well-being, nurse staffing, and compliance with care standards explain the mortality effect; financial engineering (rent, interest, fees, reduced cash) shifts resources from care to capital. This is the single most-cited "PE kills patients" empirical result and the template for the India comparison.
Corroboration: [PESP summary](https://pestakeholder.org/news/new-study-finds-that-private-equity-ownership-of-nursing-homes-substantially-increased-mortality-decreased-patient-well-being-2/) | [California Health Care Foundation](https://www.chcf.org/resource/higher-death-rates-costs-nursing-homes-private-equity/)

> **Caveat / classification nuance:** The 10–11% mortality figure is a *study result* (STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION at the level of "PE ownership increased mortality"), not a government adjudication. The "20,150 lives" and "$21B" are the authors' extrapolations (VERIFIED as *reported estimates*, but model-dependent).

## 3. Hospitals — adverse events, mortality, surgery, finances, staffing

### 3.1 Kannan, Bruch & Song — hospital-acquired conditions (the JAMA 2023 headline)
**Citation:** Sneha Kannan, Joseph Dov Bruch, Zirui Song. "Changes in Hospital Adverse Events and Patient Outcomes Associated With Private Equity Acquisition." *JAMA* **2023; 330(24):2365–2375** (Dec 26, 2023). Sample: **51 PE hospitals (662,095 hospitalizations)** vs **259 matched controls (4,160,720 hospitalizations)**, 100% Medicare Part A claims, 2009–2019.
Sources: [JAMA full text](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2813379) | [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10751598/) | [NIH summary](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/infections-falls-increased-private-equity-owned-hospitals)

| Outcome | Effect | 95% CI | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hospital-acquired conditions (composite, primary outcome) | **+25.4%** (4.6 more per 10,000; P=.004) | 2.0–7.2 | VERIFIED FACT |
| Falls | **+27.3%** (1.9 per 10,000; P=.02) | 0.3–3.4 | VERIFIED FACT |
| Central line–associated bloodstream infections | **+37.7%** (1.5 per 10,000; P=.04) | 0.4–2.6 | VERIFIED FACT |
| Central-line placements | **−16.2%** (37.1 fewer per 10,000) | — | VERIFIED FACT |
| Surgical site infections | +147.8% point estimate but **not significant** (P=.69) | −2.3 to 34.2 | VERIFIED FACT (null result) |
| In-hospital mortality | −0.2 pp (4.8% relative reduction) | −0.3 to −0.1 | VERIFIED FACT |
| Transfers to other acute care | **+12.2%** | 0.2–0.4 pp | VERIFIED FACT |

**Interpretation note (important for neutrality):** The slightly *lower* in-hospital mortality is partly attributed by the authors to PE hospitals admitting younger/lower-acuity patients and transferring sicker patients out (the 12.2% transfer increase). So "adverse events up despite a lower-risk pool" is the careful reading. Lower in-hospital death + more transfers + worse safety = quality concern, not a mortality benefit. (EXPERT INTERPRETATION of the authors' discussion.)

### 3.2 Bruch, Gondi & Song — hospital finances (the financial precursor study)
**Citation:** Bruch JD, Gondi S, Song Z. "Changes in Hospital Income, Use, and Quality Associated With Private Equity Acquisition." *JAMA Internal Medicine* 2020; 180(11):1428–1435. Compared **204 PE-acquired hospitals (2005–2017/2019)** to controls.
Source: [Google Scholar record](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup?title=Changes+in+hospital+income,+use,+and+quality+associated+with+private+equity+acquisition&author=JD+Bruch&author=S+Gondi&author=Z+Song&publication_year=2020&journal=JAMA+Intern+Med&volume=180&pages=1428-1435) | [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343847613_Changes_in_Hospital_Income_Use_and_Quality_Associated_With_Private_Equity_Acquisition)
- Findings (VERIFIED FACT, study results): **increases in annual net income, total charge per patient day, charge-to-cost ratio, and case-mix index** relative to controls; ~**1% decrease in share of Medicare discharges** (consistent with shifting toward privately insured patients). This established that PE hospitals raise charges/income — the financial side of the later quality findings.

### 3.3 Postoperative mortality — Diaz et al. (Health Affairs 2025)
**Citation:** Adrian Diaz, Mitchell Mead, Stefanie Rohde, Nicholas Kunnath, Justin B. Dimick, Andrew M. Ibrahim. "Hospitals Acquired By Private Equity Firms: Increased Postoperative Mortality For Common Inpatient Surgeries." *Health Affairs* 2025; 44(5):554–562. Sample: **67 PE hospitals vs 634 controls**, Medicare, four common general-surgery operations, difference-in-differences.
Source: [Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2024.01102)

| Finding | Figure | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| 30-day postoperative mortality | **+2.7 percentage points** vs control | VERIFIED FACT (study result) |
| Mechanism | "Failure to rescue" **+3.9 pp**; no change in complication rate | VERIFIED FACT |
| Concentration of harm | Driven by **unplanned/emergent** surgeries; no significant change for elective | VERIFIED FACT |

Note: This is the "Cerullo-adjacent" surgical-outcomes line of work; **Marcelo Cerullo's** own Health Affairs work (e.g., service-line profitability 2021, financial/operational implications 2022) found PE hospitals add *profitable* service lines (interventional cardiac cath, hemodialysis, robotic surgery) — i.e., service-mix engineering. [Cerullo service-line study](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/full/10.1377/hlthaff.2021.00541)

> **Counter-evidence (be neutral):** A separate Cerullo-et-al. cross-sectional study of **>21 million Medicare beneficiaries** with 5 acute medical conditions found PE acquisition associated with *lower* inpatient mortality (−1.1 pp) and 30-day mortality (−1.4 pp). Mortality findings across studies are mixed and condition/design-dependent; the *adverse-event* and *postoperative failure-to-rescue* findings are more consistent. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9055457/) — STRONG EVIDENCE that results are heterogeneous.

| Salary expenditures (hospital-wide) | **−16.6%** | VERIFIED FACT |
| ED salary expenditures | **−18.2%** | VERIFIED FACT |
| ICU salary expenditures | **−15.9%** | VERIFIED FACT |
| ED deaths | **+7.0 per 10,000 visits (+13.4%** from baseline 52.4) | VERIFIED FACT |

## 4. Emergency Medicine & Surprise Billing — Envision/KKR, TeamHealth/Blackstone, No Surprises Act

Physician-staffing roll-ups built business models on **out-of-network ("surprise") billing**: ER/anesthesia/radiology doctors staffed in-network hospitals but billed out-of-network, extracting premium reimbursements. The **No Surprises Act (NSA, effective Jan 1, 2022)** banned the practice and created the Independent Dispute Resolution (IDR) arbitration process, undercutting these models.

## 5. Anesthesia — FTC v. Welsh Carson / U.S. Anesthesia Partners (the roll-up test case)

**FTC complaint filed Sep 21, 2023** (FTC v. U.S. Anesthesia Partners Inc. and Welsh, Carson, Anderson & Stowe). Welsh Carson created **USAP in 2012**; the FTC alleged a three-part scheme: (1) **roll-up** of nearly every large Texas anesthesia practice (USAP "acquired more than a dozen practices"); (2) **price-setting agreements** with remaining independents; (3) a market-allocation deal to keep a significant competitor out of USAP's territory.

| Allegation / metric | Detail | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| USAP relative size — Houston | "at least 4× the second-largest group" | VERIFIED FACT (FTC allegation) |
| USAP relative size — Dallas | "6× the second-largest group" | VERIFIED FACT (FTC allegation) |
| USAP relative size — statewide | "nearly 7× the second-largest group" | VERIFIED FACT (FTC allegation) |
| UnitedHealthcare reimbursement (early 2020) | **95% above** Texas in-network median; **65% above** Houston average | VERIFIED FACT (FTC allegation) |
| Harm to Texans | "tens of millions of extra dollars" per year | VERIFIED FACT (FTC allegation) |
| Practices rolled up since 2012 | "more than a dozen" | VERIFIED FACT (FTC allegation) |

> These are **FTC allegations** (VERIFIED that the FTC alleged them), not court-adjudicated facts.

**Procedural outcome:**
- **May 13, 2024:** District court (S.D. Tex.) **dismissed Welsh Carson** from the federal case on procedural grounds (FTC Sec. 13(b) authority over a minority/holding investor), while **USAP remained** a defendant. [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/05/alerts-privateequity-hltc-district-court-grants-welsh-carsons-motion)
- **Jan 2025:** FTC announced a **settlement/consent order with Welsh Carson** (administrative). [FTC Jan 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-secures-settlement-private-equity-firm-antitrust-roll-scheme-case)
- **May 2025:** FTC approved the **final consent order (3–0 vote)**: Welsh Carson must **freeze its USAP investment** at current levels, **reduce board seats to one non-chair seat**, obtain **FTC prior approval for any US anesthesia investments**, give **prior notice of other hospital-based physician-practice investments**; order runs **10 years** with compliance reporting. [FTC final order](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/05/ftc-approves-final-order-welsh-carson) | [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/02/18/2025-02719/welsh-carson-anderson-and-stowe-analysis-of-agreement-containing-consent-order-to-aid-public-comment)
- **USAP settlement:** FTC and USAP reached a settlement in the Texas price-collusion case. [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/ftc-us-anesthesia-partners-usap-settlement-texas-welsh-carson/818406/) (Case docket showed activity continuing/stayed into 2025–2026 per the [Georgetown litigation tracker](https://litigationtracker.law.georgetown.edu/litigation/federal-trade-commission-v-u-s-anesthesia-partners-inc-et-al/).)

**Significance:** First major FTC action explicitly targeting a **PE "roll-up" / serial-acquisition** strategy assembled below HSR reporting thresholds — the central enforcement theory for PE in healthcare. The Welsh Carson dismissal also revealed a **legal gap**: holding-company/limited-partner investors can be hard to reach in federal court.

## 8. The Steward Health Care / Cerberus collapse — the sale-leaseback case study

**Mechanism (the lesson for India):** Sale-leaseback converts hospital real estate into cash (dividends to PE/management) while saddling the operating hospital with **inflated, escalating rent** it cannot afford — a structure that boosts the REIT's earnings and the PE sponsor's returns but starves the hospital, leading to service cuts, deferred maintenance, and collapse. Senate framing: described by Sen. Sanders as functionally a "Ponzi scheme"; nurse testimony described understaffing-linked patient deaths. ## 9. Cross-government policy response (FTC / DOJ / HHS) and the regulatory turn

### 9.1 The cross-government RFI
- **March 5, 2024:** FTC, DOJ Antitrust Division, and HHS jointly issued a **Request for Information on Consolidation in Health Care Markets**, covering transactions by health systems, payers, PE funds and "other alternative asset managers," explicitly including deals *below* DOJ/FTC antitrust-reporting thresholds. Comment deadline **May 6, 2024**; accompanied by a public workshop. [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/03/federal-trade-commission-department-justice-department-health-human-services-launch-cross-government) (VERIFIED FACT.)

### 9.2 The HHS/FTC/DOJ report
- **Jan 15, 2025:** HHS released the RFI-response report. Headline findings: PE acquisitions associated with **higher prices** — **hospitals +7–16%**, **physician practices +4–20%** — plus reduced access (esp. rural service cuts/closures), layoffs, inadequate staffing, and diminished quality. Over **40%** of comments mentioned PE; of comments expressing an opinion on PE, **95% were negative**. [HHS report (PDF)](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/hhs-consolidation-health-care-markets-rfi-response-report.pdf) | [HHS news](https://www.hhs.gov/about/news/2025/01/15/hhs-releases-report-consolidation-private-equity-health-care-markets.html) (VERIFIED FACT — government report findings.)

### 9.3 GAO
- **GAO-25-107450** — "Health Care Consolidation: Published Estimates of the Extent and Effects of Physician Consolidation" — synthesizes evidence that physician consolidation (incl. PE) raises prices. [GAO](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107450/index.html) (VERIFIED FACT.)

### 9.4 State laws (2025 wave)
At least **7 states** enacted PE-healthcare oversight laws in 2025: **California, Indiana, Massachusetts, Maine, New Mexico, Oregon, Washington.** [Stateline](https://stateline.org/2025/11/21/new-state-laws-tackle-private-equitys-growing-role-in-health-care/) | [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/alerts-privateequity-hltc-states-continue-to-pursue-and-expand)
- **California:** SB 351 (clinical independence) + AB 1415 (pre-close notice for "material change" transactions; OHCA Cost & Market Impact Review) — effective **Jan 1, 2026**. [PESP](https://pestakeholder.org/news/new-california-laws-regulate-private-equity-in-healthcare/)
- **Oregon:** restricts dual MSO/clinical ownership; bars MSO control over staffing, visit duration, coding, pricing, billing, payer contracting.
- **Massachusetts:** expanded reporting (PE/REIT/MSO), higher penalties, **limits on hospital–REIT sale-leasebacks** (direct Steward response).
- **Indiana:** AG authority to investigate concentration; **90-day pre-close notice**, **45-day** AG approve/deny window.
(All VERIFIED FACTS — enacted state law / reported.)

## 12. Mechanisms — how PE extracts value (synthesis for the India comparison)

1. **Leverage / debt loading** — buyout debt placed on the operating company; interest payments rose **~325%** at PE nursing homes (Gupta et al.). [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28474)
2. **Sale-leaseback / rent extraction** — real estate sold to a REIT (e.g., MPT); cash dividended out; operator left with inflated escalating rent (Steward). Lease payments **+75%** at PE nursing homes. (Steward §8.)
3. **Roll-ups / serial add-on acquisitions** — assembling local-market dominance below HSR thresholds (USAP anesthesia; 621 of 1,049 deals in 2024 were add-ons). [PESP 2024](https://pestakeholder.org/reports/healthcare-deals-2024-in-review/)
4. **Staffing cuts** — FTEs **−11.6%**, salary **−16.6%**; ED salary **−18.2%** (Annals 2025). [Annals](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/ANNALS-24-03471)
5. **Billing strategies** — out-of-network surprise billing (Envision/TeamHealth), IDR arbitration dominance (53% of disputes by 3 PE firms), upcoding/risk-coding, service-line cherry-picking (add profitable lines per Cerullo).
6. **Dividend recaps / management & monitoring fees** — fees **+7.7%** (nursing homes); ~$111M Steward dividend; ~$800M Cerberus gain.

**Net pattern:** capital is extracted (dividends, rent, interest, fees) while care inputs (staffing, supplies, maintenance) are reduced, with measurable downstream harm (infections, falls, ED/postoperative deaths) and higher prices to payers/patients/taxpayers.

## 14. Relevance to the India investigation (analyst note)

The US evidence supplies the **causal-mechanism toolkit** and the **outcome-measurement playbook** for examining foreign PE/sovereign/pension capital in Indian healthcare:
- Watch for **sale-leaseback / OpCo-PropCo / REIT** structures around Indian hospital chains (the Steward mechanism).
- Watch for **single-specialty roll-ups** (dialysis, eye-care, fertility, diagnostics, oncology day-care) assembled below CCI thresholds (the USAP/physician-practice mechanism), and apply MSA-equivalent local-market-share tests.
- Watch for **debt loading, dividend recaps, and management/monitoring fees** to offshore vehicles (Mauritius/Singapore/Netherlands) extracting cash while care inputs are cut.
- Outcome metrics to demand from Indian regulators/filings: staffing ratios, hospital-acquired-infection rates, pricing vs. comparator, and Medicare/insurance-equivalent (Ayushman Bharat/IRDAI) billing patterns and upcoding.
- The US **regulatory turn** (FTC roll-up theory; state pre-close-notice and sale-leaseback limits; cross-government RFI) is the template for what CCI/IRDAI/NHA-side oversight could adopt.
