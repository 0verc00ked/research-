## Research Cell: Agritech, Agri-Inputs, Seeds, Cold Chain & Warehousing

**Investigation:** "Foreign Private Equity and Institutional Capital in India's Healthcare, Pharmaceutical, Food and Agriculture Systems"
**Cell focus:** Map foreign institutional/PE/sovereign-wealth/pension capital across the agriculture value chain (agritech & supply, seeds & inputs, cold chain & warehousing) and document/hypothesize farmer-impact mechanisms.
**Compiled:** 2026-06-24
**Currency note:** USD↔INR conversions use the contemporaneous press-reported figures where stated; where only one currency was reported, conversions are approximate at ~INR 75/USD (2021–22 deals) to ~INR 83–84/USD (2024–25 deals) and are flagged as approximate.

## 0. Evidence Classification Legend

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **VERIFIED FACT** | Stated in a primary filing (SEBI/BSE/NSE/MCA/CCI/regulator), a company's own press release, or an investor's own press release. |
| **STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION** | Multiple independent reputable sources agree; or a primary source plus corroborating press. |
| **EXPERT INTERPRETATION** | Analysis by named experts / peer-reviewed / multilateral institutions (OECD etc.). |
| **PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS** | Logically supported mechanism, not yet demonstrated in this specific Indian context. |
| **UNPROVEN CLAIM** | Asserted by a single source / press only, not independently confirmed. |
| **press-reported** | A modifier on any deal value/stake that comes from financial press rather than a filing. |

> **Critical caveat throughout:** Ownership/control facts (who invested, how much, what stake) are documented to the best available standard below. **Causation/impact claims** (that this capital *reduced farmer bargaining power* or *raised prices*) are a SEPARATE category. For the Indian agritech segment specifically, the documented business model is generally *disintermediation* (cutting out mandi middlemen), which the companies and some sources argue *increases* farmgate realization. The "consolidation harms farmers" thesis is, for most of these targets, a **PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS** drawing on OECD/academic theory about *seed/input* concentration — it is NOT a verified outcome for the venture-stage aggregation platforms. This dossier deliberately keeps the two apart.

## 1. Overview

Foreign institutional capital has entered India's agriculture value chain through three distinct channels, each with a different risk/impact profile:

1. **Venture & growth equity into agritech/supply-chain platforms** (DeHaat, Ninjacart, WayCool, Captain Fresh, Arya.ag, Samunnati, Stellapps). Backers are global VC/growth funds (Tiger Global, Prosus/Naspers, Accel, Sofina, RTP Global, Lightrock), a sovereign-wealth fund (Temasek/Singapore), impact/DFI capital (IFC, FMO, DFC, IDH, Gates Foundation), a pension-linked manager (Nuveen/TIAA), and a strategic input multinational (Syngenta, via Syngenta Group Ventures). These are mostly *minority* stakes; control generally remains with founders, though several companies are now loss-making and capital-dependent.

2. **Large-cap private equity & sovereign-adjacent capital into seeds/inputs** (KKR and Alpha Wave Global into UPL's Advanta Enterprises; the historical Monsanto/Mahyco Bt-cotton structure now under Bayer; Fairfax-controlled NCML in grain handling). These are the deals most relevant to the "input concentration → farmer pricing power" thesis because seeds/traits are where market concentration is empirically documented (OECD 2018).

3. **Strategic & PE capital into cold chain / warehousing / collateral management** (Fairfax → NCML 74%; Temasek/Investcorp/IDFC → StarAgri; Mitsubishi/IFC/Norwest historically into Snowman, later sold to Adani; Coldman). 100% FDI is permitted on the automatic route in agri warehousing/cold storage, making this the most open segment.

**Listed-equity FPI channel:** Separately, foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) hold material minority stakes in listed seed companies — most notably **Kaveri Seed Company (KSCL)**, where FII/FPI holding rose from ~16.45% (Dec 2022) to ~20.36% (Sep 2025) [press-reported, trendlyne].

#### Mahyco Monsanto Biotech (MMB) — Bt cotton trait licensor (now Bayer)
The dominant transgenic-cotton trait conduit in India.

- **Structure:** **MMB is a 50:50 JV between Mahyco and Monsanto Investments India Pvt. Ltd. (MIIPL), now part of the Bayer group** (post Bayer's 2018 acquisition of Monsanto). [EXPERT INTERPRETATION / academic] https://law-in-action.com/2016/04/20/monsato-india-cotton-seeds/
- **Litigation:** *Monsanto Technology LLC v. Nuziveedu Seeds Ltd. & Ors.* — Delhi HC division bench held the trait unpatentable under s.3(j) Patents Act; matter went to the Supreme Court; trait-value compensation governed by government policy during pendency. [VERIFIED — multiple legal summaries] https://lawbhoomi.com/monsanto-technology-llc-v-nuziveedu-seeds-limited-ors/

### 3B. Mechanisms by which input/aggregation concentration *could* affect farmer bargaining power (theory)

The following are drawn from OECD multilateral analysis and the academic literature. They are **EXPERT INTERPRETATION** as general mechanisms and **PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESES** as applied to specific Indian targets above — NOT verified outcomes for any named venture-backed platform.

1. **Input-side market power (seeds/traits/chemistry).** Where a handful of firms control germplasm, transgenic traits, and the matched crop-protection chemistry, farmers face **higher input costs and reduced choice**. The OECD found ~80% of surveyed crop farmers reported seed-price increases over five years and ~two-thirds felt they had **less bargaining power** buying seed than before. *(EXPERT INTERPRETATION — OECD 2018 "Concentration in Seed Markets.")*
   https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/12/concentration-in-seed-markets_g1g99473/9789264308367-en.pdf
2. **Trait-plus-chemistry bundling / lock-in.** Proprietary herbicide-tolerance traits sold alongside a specific partner herbicide (e.g., the RiceTec/Savannah FullPage + ADAMA Vezir bundle; historically the Bt-cotton trait + branded-seed model) can **tie input purchases together** and raise switching costs. *(PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS as applied to FullPage; structurally analogous to OECD non-horizontal-merger concerns.)*
3. **Buyer-side ("oligopsony") power in aggregation/processing/retail.** Where downstream aggregators, processors or retailers consolidate, they can **compress farmgate prices** while protecting margins, leaving farmers "squeezed" between concentrated input sellers and concentrated buyers. *(EXPERT INTERPRETATION — OECD 2021 "Concentration and Market Power in the Food Chain.")*
   https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/02/concentration-and-market-power-in-the-food-chain_aa5064d9/3151e4ca-en.pdf
4. **Data/algorithmic asymmetry.** Platforms (and strategic input/retail co-investors such as Walmart/Syngenta on Ninjacart's cap table) accumulate granular data on farmer demand, prices and behavior, which *could* be used to optimize procurement pricing against fragmented sellers. *(PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS — no evidence of misuse on the public record.)*

### 3C. The counter-thesis (kept explicit for neutrality)

- The **stated business model** of DeHaat/Ninjacart/WayCool/Arya/Captain Fresh is **disintermediation** — removing layers of mandi middlemen. Indian press/policy sources cite that traditional mandi chains can leave farmers as little as **30–40% of retail price**, and that direct/digital trade can raise per-kg realization by **25–40%**; e-NAM (1,656 mandis, ~1.8 cr farmers, ~INR 4.84 lakh cr traded) is the public-sector analogue. *(press-reported — TheDeepSoil; IndianWeb2/e-NAM.)* **PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS** that aggregation *improves* farmer realization, at least initially.
  https://thedeepsoil.com/direct-farm-trade-fair-prices-farmers-india/ ; https://www.indianweb2.com/2026/04/enam-builds-indias-ist-truly-digital.html
- **The unresolved empirical question** (and the crux of a parliamentary/antitrust inquiry): *whether, once such platforms achieve scale and their foreign capital seeks returns, the early pro-farmer pricing persists or inverts into oligopsony*. This is **NOT answered** by current evidence and should be framed as the central testable hypothesis, not a finding.
