# India-Specific Empirical Impact Evidence: Corporate/PE Hospital & Diagnostics Ownership Effects on Price, Quality, and Access

**Research cell:** India empirical impact evidence hunt
**Parent investigation:** "Foreign Private Equity and Institutional Capital in India's Healthcare, Pharmaceutical, Food and Agriculture Systems"
**Compiled:** 2026-06-24
**Scope:** India-specific empirical evidence (peer-reviewed studies, regulator findings, documented incidents) on the effects of corporate and private-equity (PE) hospital and diagnostics ownership on PRICE, QUALITY, and ACCESS.

---

## 0. Critical Methodological Caveat (read first)

Almost all of the evidence below documents harms in the **corporate/private hospital sector broadly**. Very little of it isolates **PE-ownership-specific** or **foreign-capital-specific** causation. The chain of inference is:

1. **VERIFIED:** Specific overcharging / unnecessary-procedure / fraud incidents occurred at named corporate or private hospitals (strong, multiply-sourced).
2. **VERIFIED:** Foreign PE / sovereign / institutional capital owns or controls several of the largest corporate hospital and diagnostics chains (filing- and CCI-grade).
3. **STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION:** The private sector systematically performs more unnecessary procedures (C-sections, hysterectomies) and charges far above benchmark rates than the public sector — driven by supplier-induced demand / financial incentives.
4. **PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS (NOT PROVEN):** That PE/foreign ownership *specifically* worsens these outcomes more than domestic private ownership. The financialisation literature argues for a mechanism (revenue targets, 3–7yr exit cycles, consolidation) but **no India study isolates PE-owned vs domestically-owned private hospital outcomes** with a clean counterfactual. The strongest single academic source (Hunter et al. 2025) is **qualitative**, not a causal-quantitative design.

This caveat must accompany any use of this dossier. Do not present incident-level facts as proof of PE-specific systemic causation.

## 1. Overview of the Evidence Landscape

India's healthcare market is dominated by the private sector, which serves roughly **68% of urban and 58% of rural in-patient care** (NPPA analysis cited by IndiaSpend). Between **April 2000 and June 2019, hospitals and diagnostic centres attracted ~USD 6.3 billion in FDI** under the 100%-automatic-route regime opened in 2000 (Hunter et al. 2025, *Sociology of Health & Illness*). Five major chains (Apollo, Fortis, Narayana, Max, Healthcare Global) reported combined revenues of **INR 129 billion (~USD 2.0 billion) in March 2017, an 80% rise over five years** (same source).

Several of the largest chains are now under foreign sovereign/PE/institutional control or have very high foreign-institutional shareholding (see Transactions Table, Section 8). Against this backdrop, the empirical record of harms falls into six buckets, detailed below:

- **(A)** Regulator (NPPA) findings of extreme mark-ups on drugs/consumables at named corporate hospitals.
- **(B)** Peer-reviewed evidence of supplier-induced demand: unnecessary C-sections and hysterectomies concentrated in the private sector.
- **(C)** COVID-era overcharging — large-N bill audits in Maharashtra.
- **(D)** AB-PMJAY (Ayushman Bharat) fraud and de-empanelment data, including fatal unnecessary-procedure cases.
- **(E)** Documented ethics/negligence incidents at corporate hospitals (Fortis Adya Singh, Max Shalimar Bagh, Medanta).
- **(F)** The financialisation-of-Indian-healthcare academic literature (mechanism, not causation).
- **(G)** CGHS/ECHS rate disputes and empanelment suspensions (a structural friction, not a harm per se).

## 2. (A) Regulator Findings — NPPA Mark-Up Analysis (2017–2018)

The **National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA)** analysed bills from **four "reputed" NCR private hospitals** after bereaved relatives complained of overcharging. This is the single most authoritative *regulator-grade* evidence of price gouging.

- The four families were charged a **total of ~INR 69 lakh (~USD 105,984), averaging >INR 17 lakh (~USD 26,112) each** (NPPA analysis, reported by IndiaSpend, 2018). — **VERIFIED FACT (regulator finding, press-reported figures).**
- **Mark-ups found by NPPA on consumables/drugs (purchase price → billed price):** — **VERIFIED FACT (regulator finding).**
  - Three-way stop cock: **1,737%** (bought ~INR 5.77, charged ~INR 106)
  - IV infusion set: **1,271%**
  - Disposable syringe: **1,208%**
  - Non-scheduled drug *Adrenor*: **1,192%**
  - Scheduled (price-controlled) drug *Propofol*: **357%**
- **Bill composition:** drugs/devices/diagnostics = **56%** of total bills; non-scheduled medicines = **25.6%**; scheduled medicines = **4.1%**; diagnostics = **15%**. — **VERIFIED FACT.**
- NPPA concluded hospitals buy in bulk cheaply and bill patients far above MRP; **margins up to 1,737%** were the headline. The NPPA chairman (Bhupendra Singh) was **transferred 8 days after / around the time of the report (transfer order ~1 March 2018)** — widely read as a chilling signal, though causation is **UNPROVEN CLAIM**. — Mark-ups VERIFIED; transfer-as-retaliation is INTERPRETATION.
- Source: IndiaSpend, "Hospitals Inflated Drug Prices By Up To 1,737%..." — https://www.indiaspend.com/hospitals-inflated-drug-prices-by-up-to-1737-govt-regulator-8-days-later-hes-transferred-92425/
- Corroboration: Down To Earth, "Measures to curb 'unethical profiteering'..." — https://www.downtoearth.org.in/health/government-must-take-action-against-systematic-loot-of-private-hospitals-patients-59771

### Stent price-cap context (Feb 2017)
- NPPA capped coronary stent prices, cutting them **~80%**: bare-metal/regular stents to **INR 7,260 (~USD 111)** and drug-eluting stents to **INR 29,600 (~USD 454)**; channel margin (hospital + distributor) capped at **8%**. — **VERIFIED FACT (regulator order).**
- NPPA had earlier observed "huge unethical mark-ups... at each stage in the supply chain of coronary stents, resulting in irrational, restrictive and exorbitant prices." — **VERIFIED FACT (regulator language).**
- **Documented unintended consequence:** after the cap, hospitals raised consumables/procedure/doctor/lab charges to recoup margin, so total bills did not fall proportionately. — **STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION** (multiple reports + a peer-reviewed *Social Science & Medicine* study on the price-control policy's unintended consequences, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0277953619307324).
- Sources: The Wire — https://thewire.in/business/overcharged-cardiac-stent-npaa ; Down To Earth — https://www.downtoearth.org.in/governance/nppa-puts-price-cap-on-cardiac-stents-says-hospitals-overcharge-57057 ; Medical Dialogues — https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/medical-devices/govt-reaffirms-nppa-stent-price-caps-states-tasked-with-curbing-hospital-overcharging-164776

## 3. (B) Peer-Reviewed Supplier-Induced Demand: Unnecessary Procedures

### B1. Caesarean sections — the strongest quantitative private-vs-public evidence

**Study: "An epidemic of avoidable caesarean deliveries in the private sector in India: Is physician-induced demand at play?"** — *Social Science & Medicine* (2020), cross-sectional NFHS analysis with logistic regression + propensity score matching. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33203552/ ; ScienceDirect: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0277953620307309

- Odds ratio for C-section in private vs public facilities **rose from 1.62 (NFHS-3, 2005–06) to 4.17 (NFHS-4, 2015–16)**. — **VERIFIED FACT (peer-reviewed).**
- **~1.83 million avoidable C-sections** would be eliminated, with **~USD 320.6 million potential savings**, if the private sector met the WHO 15% threshold. — **VERIFIED FACT (study estimate).**
- Authors conclude **physician-induced demand from perverse financial incentives** in the private sector is at play; rising rates "cannot be explained by medical reasons alone." — **STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION (peer-reviewed).**

**IIM-Ahmedabad working paper: "Too much care? Private health care sector and surgical interventions during childbirth in India"** — https://ideas.repec.org/p/iim/iimawp/14595.html ; reported by Business Standard ("~900,000 unnecessary C-sections in a year," 2018) https://www.business-standard.com/article/current-affairs/india-s-private-hospitals-saw-900k-unnecessary-c-sections-in-a-year-study-118120200214_1.html

- **~900,000 "preventable" unplanned C-sections** out of ~7 million private-hospital deliveries in one year. — **STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION (academic working paper, press-reported).**
- Women in private facilities **13.5–14 percentage points more likely** to undergo an unplanned C-section vs public. — **VERIFIED FACT (study).**
- Public–private C-section gap **grew from ~5% (NFHS-1) to ~29% (NFHS-4)**. — **VERIFIED FACT.**
- NFHS cost signal: natural birth in private ~**INR 10,814** vs C-section ~**INR 23,978** (financial incentive). — **VERIFIED FACT (NFHS-derived).**

**NFHS-5 (2019–21) rates** (via ORF analysis, https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/the-c-section-surge-in-india-uncovering-the-impact-of-profit-on-childbirth):
- Private hospitals: **47.4%** of births by C-section vs public **14.3%**. — **VERIFIED FACT (NFHS-5).**
- Private OOPE for C-section ~**USD 498 (~INR 43,000)** vs public ~**USD 99 (~INR 8,600)**. — **VERIFIED FACT.**
- WHO/evidence threshold: rates above ~**15–19%** show no maternal/neonatal mortality benefit. — **VERIFIED FACT (WHO position).**

> **Caveat:** These studies compare *private vs public*, NOT *PE-owned vs non-PE private*. They are strong evidence of private-sector profit-driven overuse, but do NOT isolate a PE/foreign-ownership effect.

### B2. Unnecessary hysterectomies (insurance-driven)

- A PIL by Dr Narendra Gupta alleged a "widespread pattern of doctors performing medically unnecessary hysterectomies to reap high insurance fees" in **Bihar, Rajasthan, Chhattisgarh**. — **VERIFIED FACT (litigation record).**
- **~16,000 women in Bihar (2011)** underwent hysterectomies to claim RSBY insurance benefits; **~70% of all hysterectomies occur in private facilities**; documented cases of same-day surgery after only a sonography. — **STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION / VERIFIED incidents** (Prayas / NGO investigations, Supreme Court directed states to adopt central monitoring guidelines).
- Sources: Global Health Check — http://www.globalhealthcheck.org/?p=1374 ; Quartz — https://qz.com/india/515588/doctors-in-india-are-shamelessly-lying-to-women-to-perform-unwarranted-hysterectomies ; Drishti IAS overview — https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/hysterectomy

### B3. Standardised-patient & systematic-review evidence on private-sector quality

- PLOS Medicine systematic review (Basu et al.) of LMIC public vs private: private-sector providers **more frequently violated medical standards of practice and had poorer patient outcomes**, but offered greater timeliness/hospitality; private-sector efficiency was lower partly due to **perverse incentives for unnecessary testing/treatment.** — **STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION (peer-reviewed systematic review).** https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pmed.1001244
- TB standardised-patient study, Mumbai (BMJ/peer-reviewed): documented private-sector diagnosis/management gaps vs public. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9582305/ — **VERIFIED FACT (peer-reviewed).**

## 4. (C) COVID-Era Overcharging — Large-N Bill Audits (Maharashtra)

**Peer-reviewed / SATHI study: "Overcharging by Private Hospitals during the COVID Pandemic in India: A Patient-based Analysis of Rate Regulation"** (Shweta Marathe, SATHI Pune). India Forum write-up: https://www.theindiaforum.in/forum/overcharging-during-pandemic-private-hospitals-maharashtra ; ResearchGate full text: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371315885

- Method: **100 patient/relative interviews + 12 stakeholder interviews + bill-by-bill analysis of 120 hospitalisation episodes across 15 of Maharashtra's 37 districts** (Nov 2021–Feb 2022). — **VERIFIED (methodology).**
- **82.5% of hospitalisation episodes involved overcharging** vs official capped rates. — **VERIFIED FACT (study).**
- For **77 critical-care episodes, average daily medical bills were 400–500% higher** than official rates. — **VERIFIED FACT.**
- ICU+ventilator official cap = **INR 9,000/day** (all-inclusive); hospitals unbundled and billed each item separately. — **VERIFIED FACT.**
- **Large hospitals over-charged at higher frequency and scale** than medium/small. — **VERIFIED FACT (study finding).** [Note: a size effect, NOT explicitly an ownership/PE effect.]

**Government audit data (Maharashtra, 2021):**
- Health Minister order **11 Nov 2021** to audit bills and recover excess. — **VERIFIED FACT.**
- **BMC auditors screened 6,851 bills worth ~INR 112 crore and forced reductions/refunds of ~INR 14 crore.** — **VERIFIED FACT (municipal audit).** (One source rounds to "6,000 bills.")
- **Thane: special audit of 17 private hospitals found ~INR 1.82 crore overcharged.** — **VERIFIED FACT.**
- Statewide informal estimate of total overcharging: **INR 1,000–9,000 crore** (wide range; ~75% / 1,954 of audited cases overcharged; average ~INR 21,215/day, ~3x official ICU rate). — **PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS / press-reported estimate** (the crore range is an extrapolation, not an audited total).
- Sources: AID India — https://aidindia.org/private-hospitals-refund-excess-bills-for-covid-care-in-maharashtra/ ; Medical Dialogues (BMC 6,000 bills) — https://medicaldialogues.in/state-news/maharashtra/overcharging-by-private-hospitals-bmc-auditors-screen-6000-bills-worth-rs-112-crore-70759 ; Scroll (Thane) — https://scroll.in/latest/971251/coronavirus-17-private-hospitals-in-thane-overcharged-patients-rs-1-82-crore-finds-audit ; The Leaflet — https://theleaflet.in/regulation-of-private-hospitals-during-covid-gets-a-booster-of-social-accountability/

## 7. (F) Financialisation-of-Indian-Healthcare Literature (Mechanism Evidence)

**Hunter, Chakravarthi, Marathe & Murray (2025), "Financialisation and the Reshaping of Private Healthcare: A Case Study in India," *Sociology of Health & Illness* (Wiley).** This is the single most directly on-point academic source linking PE/financial capital to Indian healthcare restructuring. **It is QUALITATIVE** (policy docs + 2 witness seminars + 45 interviews in Mumbai/Pune, Oct 2017–Dec 2018, including 2 PE investors). PMC full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12082753/ ; Wiley: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-9566.70041

Key findings (all **EXPERT INTERPRETATION / STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION** from qualitative data unless noted):
- **FDI into hospitals/diagnostics ~USD 6.3 billion (Apr 2000–Jun 2019)**; global healthcare acquisitions hit **~USD 60 billion in 2023**. — **VERIFIED (cited official/industry figures).**
- PE operates on **3–7 year investment cycles**; chains expand by **acquiring debt-distressed hospitals** ("you take the hospital, service my loan") rather than greenfield build-out. — **EXPERT INTERPRETATION (interview-based).**
- Hospital departments run on **monthly revenue targets** with management incentives; one administrator: *"they don't bother about anything [else], just increase the revenue."* Unprofitable wards/services get closed. — **EXPERT INTERPRETATION.**
- **Consolidation harms small providers:** Bombay Nursing Homes Association reported **~1/5 of its 650 registered nursing homes shut by 2012**; insurance reimbursement structures favour large players. — **VERIFIED / STRONG.**
- Maharashtra had **42 private corporate hospitals in 2015** (23 Mumbai, 18 Pune); corporate hospitals ~10% of total across major metros by 2010. — **VERIFIED FACT.**
- **Regulatory gap:** Clinical Establishments Act unadopted by most states; **no requirement to disclose investor details**; NABH accreditation may "segment and squeeze out" smaller providers. — **EXPERT INTERPRETATION.**

**Named PE/foreign investors and targets identified in the paper** (Maharashtra-centric): Asian Healthcare Fund→Wellspring; Tata Healthcare Fund→Lokmanya (Pune); Sabre Partners→Oyster & Pearl, Thyrocare; ICICI Ventures & IDFC Alternatives→Sahyadri; **Everstone→Sahyadri controlling stake (2019)**; **OrbiMed→Surya Mother & Child (2013)**; **SeaLink Capital→acquired OrbiMed's stake (2018)**; **IHH→Fortis (INR 40 bn / ~USD 1.1 bn)**. — **VERIFIED FACTS (deal-level).**

Related: "Financial Viability of Private Hospitals Operating Under AB PM-JAY" — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12364732/

## 8. (G) CGHS / ECHS Rate Disputes & Empanelment Suspensions (Structural Friction)

This is evidence of *price/rate tension* between corporate hospitals and government schemes — a structural pressure point, not a patient harm per se.

- CGHS package rates **not revised since 2014**; private hospitals say rates fail to cover salary increments and rising consumables costs. — **VERIFIED FACT (industry position).**
- CGHS reportedly takes **9–12 months to clear bills** vs the contractual 70% within 5 working days / balance within 30 days — working-capital stress. — **VERIFIED (reported).**
- **Dec 2019:** IMA warned private hospitals may **suspend CGHS/ECHS cashless services over unpaid dues.** — **VERIFIED FACT.**
- CGHS suspensions/revocations occur (e.g., **15 Jan 2024** order revoking suspension of S.S. Hospital & Research Centre, Patna). — **VERIFIED FACT.**
- Sources: Business Standard — https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/non-payment-of-cghs-echs-dues-may-force-pvt-hospitals-to-suspend-cashless-services-ima-119121901241_1.html ; Medical Dialogues — https://medicaldialogues.in/news/health/private-hospital-opposes-removal-of-payment-clause-in-proposed-cghs-empanelment-pact-99936 ; G-Connect — https://www.gconnect.in/news/cghs-empanelled-hospitals-unpaid-dues-cashless-services.html

## 11. Gaps / Unverified / What This Dossier Does NOT Establish

1. **No clean PE-vs-non-PE causal study.** No located India study compares outcomes/prices at **PE-owned** vs **non-PE domestic-private** hospitals with a counterfactual. The PE-causation claim remains a **PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS** supported by mechanism evidence (Hunter et al., qualitative) but not by quantitative attribution. **This is the single biggest evidentiary gap.**
2. **Temporal mismatch on flagship incidents.** The most cited harm incidents (Fortis Adya 2017, Max Shalimar Bagh 2017, Medanta 2017) **pre-date** the foreign-PE control events (IHH→Fortis Nov 2018; KKR→Max 2018–19). They evidence corporate-hospital conduct, not PE-era conduct.
3. **AB-PMJAY / NHA data is not ownership-disaggregated.** De-empanelment, suspension, penalty and fraud figures do not separate PE-owned, listed-with-high-FII, or independent private hospitals.
4. **COVID overcharging "INR 1,000–9,000 crore" statewide total** is an extrapolation, NOT an audited figure. Only the audited subsets (BMC ~INR 14 cr refunds on ~INR 112 cr screened; Thane ~INR 1.82 cr) are VERIFIED.
5. **Shareholding percentages** are press/aggregator snapshots; must be reconciled to the exact quarterly **BSE/NSE shareholding-pattern filings** and primary CCI orders before any authoritative use.
6. **Max Shalimar Bagh final disposition** (post-Feb 2018 stay) not fully traced — whether the licence was permanently restored, the hospital re-registered, or any final court order issued remains an open item.
7. **Vehicle/jurisdiction granularity** confirmed for KKR (Kayak Investments Holding Pte, Singapore) and Temasek/Sheares (Singapore), and IHH (Malaysia/SGX). Mauritius/Cayman layering for TPG, Everstone, OrbiMed funds is **typical but not individually filing-verified here** — flagged as PLAUSIBLE.
8. **NPPA chairman transfer as retaliation** is INTERPRETATION, not established fact.
9. **CCI orders** (e.g., the IHH-Fortis combination order; any Manipal/Temasek or KKR/Max combination notices) were referenced via press; the **primary CCI order texts were not fetched** in this pass and should be pulled for filing-grade citation.
10. **Direct PE-attributable price increases** (e.g., tariff hikes at a specific hospital before vs after a PE acquisition) — no clean before/after price dataset was located. A high-value future research target.
