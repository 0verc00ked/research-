**Research cell:** FDI policy + drug/device price-control timeline for Hospitals, Pharmaceuticals, Medical Devices, Multi-/Single-Brand Retail, Food-Product Retail, E-commerce, and Insurance.
**Investigation:** "Foreign Private Equity and Institutional Capital in India's Healthcare, Pharmaceutical, Food and Agriculture Systems."
**Compiled:** 2026-06-24
**Purpose:** A standalone, dated, primary-source-anchored regulatory chronology that the deal-census cells (hospitals, pharma, medtech, packaged food, etc.) can cross-reference to establish *what the legal/regulatory perimeter was* at the moment each foreign-capital transaction occurred.

## 1. Overview & key findings

This cell maps two parallel regulatory tracks that together define the *market structure* into which foreign PE, sovereign-wealth and institutional capital entered India's health-food-agri systems:

1. **FDI liberalization track** — a multi-decade loosening of foreign-ownership caps and a shift from prior-approval (FIPB/Government route) to the automatic route. The net direction across every sector relevant to this investigation has been **toward more foreign ownership with less prior scrutiny**, with two notable counter-moves: (a) the 2011 re-imposition of approval-route scrutiny on *brownfield* pharma (acquisitions of existing Indian drug makers), and (b) the April 2020 Press Note 3 land-border restriction (aimed at China). Even the brownfield-pharma counter-move was substantially walked back in 2016 (74% automatic).

2. **Price-control track** — the Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013 (DPCO 2013) and the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA), which moved from a cost-based to a *market-based* ceiling-price regime, then progressively extended price control beyond drugs into **medical devices** (coronary stents 2017, knee implants 2017) and into **trade-margin rationalization** (anti-cancer drugs 2019, COVID devices 2021). This is the principal regulatory force that *constrains the margins* foreign-PE-owned device and pharma platforms can extract.

**Headline conclusions (evidence-classified):**

- **VERIFIED FACT** — **Hospitals**: 100% FDI under the automatic route has been the position since the early-2000s liberalization (commonly dated to January 2000); it is the most permanently open of all the sectors here. No price cap applies to hospital *services* (only to specific consumables/devices used inside them, e.g. stents).
- **VERIFIED FACT** — **Pharma brownfield**: the regime tightened in 2011 (Press Note 3 of 2011, brownfield moved to FIPB/approval route), added conditions in January 2014, then was liberalized to **74% automatic / above-74% approval** for brownfield by Press Note 5 of 2016 (Cabinet ex-post-facto approval of the 20 June 2016 changes). Greenfield pharma stayed 100% automatic throughout.
- **VERIFIED FACT** — **Medical devices** were carved out of the pharma FDI regime to **100% automatic (greenfield and brownfield), with no non-compete restriction**, by a Cabinet decision dated **26 December 2014**, notified as **Press Note 2 (2015 Series)**, effective ~21 January 2015.
- **VERIFIED FACT** — **Multi-brand retail (MBRT)**: 51% FDI under the **Government (approval) route** with onerous conditions (US$100m minimum, 50% in back-end, 30% local sourcing from small industries, no e-commerce), via **Press Note 5 of 2012** (20 September 2012). It remains 51%/Government-route and was largely a dead letter — most foreign multi-brand retailers never entered.
- **VERIFIED FACT** — **Food-product retail trading**: announced in the **Union Budget 2016** (29 Feb 2016) and operationalized in the **20 June 2016** FDI batch — **100% FDI** for "trading, including through e-commerce, in respect of food products manufactured and/or produced in India." **DOCUMENTED DISCREPANCY**: the 2016 Cabinet release language indexed here says "automatic route" for food products, whereas the Budget announcement, the consolidated FDI policy, and all subsequent DPIIT clarifications (June 2018) treat it as the **Government (approval) route**. This file documents both and flags it.
- **VERIFIED FACT** — **E-commerce**: 100% automatic in the *marketplace* model, but *inventory-based* B2C e-commerce is prohibited for FDI; **Press Note 2 of 2018** (26 December 2018, effective 1 February 2019) tightened the marketplace rules (no marketplace control over inventory; deemed-control if a seller buys >25% from the marketplace/group).
- **VERIFIED FACT** — **Insurance** rose **26% → 49%** (Insurance Laws (Amendment) Act, 2015; President's assent 20 March 2015), **49% → 74%** (Insurance (Amendment) Act, 2021; assent 25 March 2021; in force 1 April 2021), and **74% → 100%** (Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Bill/Act, 2025; passed by Parliament 17 December 2025; most provisions in force 5 February 2026).
- **VERIFIED FACT** — **Price control**: DPCO 2013 (S.O. 1221(E), 15 May 2013) introduced **market-based** ceiling prices (simple average of all brands with ≥1% market share) for NLEM-scheduled drugs; NPPA capped **coronary stents** (S.O. 412(E), 13 Feb 2017, up to ~85% cut) and **knee implants** (S.O. 2668(E), 16 Aug 2017, ~59-69% cuts) using **Para 19 extraordinary powers**; and launched **trade-margin rationalization** on 42 anti-cancer drugs (Feb 2019, ~30% margin cap) and COVID devices (2021, 70% margin cap).

## 2. FDI track — sector-by-sector chronology

### 2.1 Hospitals & diagnostic centres — 100% automatic (long-standing)

- **Status / instrument:** FDI up to **100% under the automatic route** in hospitals (and diagnostic centres). Commonly dated to the **January 2000** liberalization that placed the sector on the automatic route; it has remained 100% automatic since.
  - Classification: **VERIFIED FACT** (the 100%-automatic status is uncontested across government and industry sources). The precise "January 2000" origin date is **STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION** (widely repeated, including in ISID's working paper and industry/legal summaries, but not re-verified here against the original 2000 Press Note).
  - Sources: Law.asia, "India's hospital sector attracts huge FDI," https://law.asia/fdi-india-hospital-sector/ ; ISID Working Paper 181, "Foreign Investment in Hospital Sector in India," https://isid.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/WP181.pdf ; India Health Exhibition, "FDI Entry Routes Into India," https://www.indiahealth-exhibition.com/fdi-entry-routes-into-india
- **Significance to this investigation:** Hospitals were never a binding FDI constraint — IHH/Fortis, KKR, TPG, Temasek, GIC, CDPQ, OTPP etc. could (and did) acquire control/large stakes in Indian hospital chains under the automatic route. The binding constraints on hospital deals are CCI merger control (e.g., IHH-Fortis) and, indirectly, the device price caps that compress procedure margins, **not** the FDI cap.
- **Price control on hospitals:** No ceiling on hospital *service* charges nationally. NPPA's stent/knee actions explicitly targeted the hospital/distributor mark-up layer (see §3). (VERIFIED FACT.)

### 2.2 Pharmaceuticals — the brownfield tightening (2011) and partial reversal (2016)

The single most policy-contested FDI area in this investigation, because brownfield = *foreign acquisition of existing Indian drug makers* (the "takeover of Indian generics" concern).

| Date | Instrument | Change | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-2011 | (Consolidated FDI policy) | 100% FDI automatic for drugs & pharmaceuticals (greenfield and brownfield), limited exceptions. | VERIFIED FACT |
| **8 Nov 2011** | **Press Note 3 (2011 Series)** | Split greenfield vs brownfield. **Greenfield: 100% automatic. Brownfield: 100% but under FIPB/Government approval route.** Motivated by a spate of foreign takeovers of domestic pharma and concerns over essential-medicine availability, R&D, and technology. | VERIFIED FACT |
| **8 Jan 2014** | **Press Note 1 (2014 Series)** | Retained 100% (greenfield automatic / brownfield approval) and added the condition that **non-compete clauses are not permitted except in special circumstances with FIPB approval** — so that Indian promoters can keep making generics. | VERIFIED FACT |
| **26 Dec 2014 (Cabinet) → Press Note 2 of 2015** | Medical-device carve-out | Medical devices removed from the pharma regime to **100% automatic** (see §2.3). | VERIFIED FACT |
| **20 Jun 2016 (Cabinet ex-post-facto) → Press Note 5 (2016 Series), 24 Jun 2016** | Brownfield liberalization | **Brownfield pharma: up to 74% FDI under the automatic route; above 74% under Government approval.** Greenfield stays 100% automatic. | VERIFIED FACT |
| **17 Apr 2020** | **Press Note 3 (2020 Series)** | Land-border restriction: any FDI from an entity of a country sharing a land border with India (or with a beneficial owner there) requires Government approval — applies across sectors incl. pharma. Aimed at opportunistic COVID-era acquisitions (esp. China). | VERIFIED FACT |

**Conditions on brownfield pharma FDI (from the 2014/2016 framework, both automatic and approval routes):**
- Maintain the production level of **National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM)** drugs and/or consumables and their supply to the domestic market at the level prevailing at the time of FDI induction;
- Maintain **R&D expenses** (in absolute/value terms) for 5 years at the level at the time of induction;
- Furnish complete information on **transfer of technology**;
- **Non-compete clause not permitted** except with Government/FIPB approval in special circumstances.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT** (these conditions are reproduced consistently in the PIB release and multiple law-firm summaries).

### 2.3 Medical devices — carve-out to 100% automatic (2014/2015)

- **Cabinet decision date:** **26 December 2014**. **Notified:** **Press Note 2 (2015 Series)** dated **6 January 2015**, **effective 21 January 2015**.
- **Change:** Medical devices carved out of the pharmaceutical FDI regime; **100% FDI under the automatic route for both greenfield and brownfield**, with FDI **restricted to manufacturing**, and the **non-compete restriction (which had applied to pharma) explicitly removed** for devices — on the rationale that India is substantially import-dependent for devices, so protecting Indian generic manufacturing is "not relevant" to devices.
- **Definition (from the Cabinet text):** a medical device is an "instrument, apparatus, appliance, implant... including software" intended for diagnosis/prevention/monitoring/treatment/alleviation of disease, injury or handicap, or investigation/replacement/modification/support of anatomy or physiological process, that **does not achieve its primary intended action by pharmacological/immunological/metabolic means**. (VERIFIED FACT.)
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT.**
- **Sources:** PMIndia, "Review of the policy on FDI in Pharmaceutical Sector – carve out for medical devices," https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/review-of-the-policy-on-foreign-direct-investment-in-pharmaceutical-sector-carve-out-for-medical-devices/ (primary; 403 on direct fetch) ; IBEF mirror of the same release (date confirmed 26 Dec 2014, text quoted), https://www.ibef.org/news/review-of-the-policy-on-foreign-direct-investment-in-pharmaceutical-sector ; India Law News, "Introduction of Separate Regulatory Framework for Medical Devices" (PN2/2015, eff. 21 Jan 2015), https://indialawnews.org/2015/04/13/introduction-of-separate-regulatory-framework-for-medical-devices/ ; Down To Earth, "Modi Cabinet approves 100% FDI in medical devices sector via automatic route," https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/modi-cabinet-approves-100-fdi-in-medical-devices-sector-via-automatic-route-47960
- **Significance:** This is the FDI provision under which Warburg Pincus (Meril/Micro Life Sciences), Morgan Stanley PE Asia (SMT), KKR (Healthium), ADIA, Temasek etc. took stakes in Indian device makers — 100% automatic, with the ability to impose non-competes on founders, with no prior approval. (Cross-ref: `medtech_pharmacy_distribution.md`.) (STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION linking the regulatory perimeter to the deal census.)

**Multi-brand retail (MBRT):**
- **Instrument:** **Press Note 5 (2012 Series), dated 20 September 2012.** **51% FDI under the Government (approval) route.**
- **Conditions (all VERIFIED FACT):**
  - Minimum FDI of **US$100 million**;
  - **At least 50% of the first US$100m** to be invested in **back-end infrastructure** (cold chain, logistics, processing, warehousing) within 3 years;
  - **30% of the value of procurement** of manufactured/processed products to be **sourced from Indian "small industries"** (plant & machinery ≤ US$1 million investment);
  - Stores only in cities with population > 1 million (or as states permit); **state governments may opt in/out**;
  - **Retail trading by e-commerce NOT permitted** for companies with MBRT FDI.
- **Status:** Remains 51%/Government route. **Largely unused** — political opposition and the state opt-in structure deterred entry; Walmart's eventual India play came via the **Flipkart** (e-commerce marketplace) route in 2018, *not* MBRT.

### 2.5 Food-product retail trading — the 2016 "Made-in-India food" channel

This is the legally distinct channel most relevant to the **packaged-food / food-system** arm of this investigation, because it allows **100% foreign ownership of retail selling Indian-made food** even though general multi-brand retail is capped at 51%.

- **Budget announcement:** Union Budget 2016-17 speech (**29 February 2016**), FM Arun Jaitley: 100% FDI to be allowed, through the FIPB/approval route, in **marketing of food products manufactured/produced in India**. (VERIFIED FACT — Budget speech / contemporaneous press.)
- **Operationalized:** in the **20 June 2016** FDI liberalization batch (Cabinet ex-post-facto approval), incorporated into the **Consolidated FDI Policy 2016** (issued 7 June 2016) and notified via the 2016 Press Note series.
- **Provision:** **100% FDI** in "trading, including through e-commerce, in respect of food products manufactured and/or produced in India." This is a deliberate **carve-out from the MBRT prohibition on e-commerce and the 51% cap** — for Indian-made food only.
- **ROUTE DISCREPANCY (documented):**
  - The indexed text of the **20 June 2016 PMIndia Cabinet release** says food products are allowed under the **"automatic route."**
  - However, the **Budget speech, the consolidated FDI policy entry, and every DPIIT clarification since (notably the 20 June 2018 clarification)** treat food-product retail trading as the **Government (approval) route**.
  - Resolution (now **VERIFIED FACT** — discrepancy resolved): the **operative, enduring position is the Government (approval) route.** This is confirmed by (a) **Para 5.2.5.2 of the Consolidated FDI Policy Circular of 2017**, (b) the **DPIIT FDI Policy Clarification on Food Product Retail Trading** (20 Jun 2018), and (c) the **Ministry of Food Processing Industries' own statement: "100% FDI (Government approval route) is permitted for trading of food products manufactured/produced in India."** The "automatic route" wording in the indexed 20 June 2016 PMIndia batch release is therefore a generalized batch-summary/drafting artifact, not the operative sectoral rule. **This file uses Government-route as the operative, verified classification.**
  - Confirming sources: DPIIT clarification PDF, https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/95d836222f2c740ec416d73551351734.pdf ; MoFPI (Government-approval-route statement), https://www.facebook.com/MOFPIIndia/posts/did-you-know100-fdi-government-approval-route-is-permitted-for-trading-of-food-p/1300133278811520/ ; MoFPI food-processing FDI page, https://foodprocessingindia.gov.in/information/details/foreign-direct-investment
- **20 June 2018 DPIIT clarification (VERIFIED FACT):** A company with FDI in food-product retail trading may **also** trade in non-food products **provided** (a) the food-retail business is kept **distinct and separate** from other businesses; (b) **inventory** of the food-retail business is **physically separated and readily distinguishable** (shared warehousing/manpower allowed); and (c) (per press summaries) separate billing. This was the change that let players like Amazon (via a JV/"Amazon Retail India") and Grofers/BigBasket-type entities structure food-retail FDI alongside other lines.
- **Sources:** Sarkaritel (Jaitley Budget 2016 quote), https://www.sarkaritel.com/100-percent-foreign-equity-in-retail-of-domestic-food-products-jaitley/ ; Franchise India, https://www.franchiseindia.com/insights/en/article/new-policy-allows-100-fdi-in-stores-that-sell-locally-produced-farm-goods.50151 ; India Infoline (food stocks 29 Feb 2016), https://www.indiainfoline.com/article/news-top-story/union-budget-2016-food-processing-stocks-surge-on-100-fdi-allowed-116022900145_1.html ; PMIndia, "Cabinet approves simplification and liberalisation of the FDI Policy, 2016 in various sectors" (20 Jun 2016 batch), https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/cabinet-approves-simplification-and-liberalisation-of-the-fdi-policy-2016-in-various-sectors/ (primary; 403 direct, fetched via search index) ; DPIIT, FDI Policy Clarification on Food Product Retail Trading (20 Jun 2018), https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/95d836222f2c740ec416d73551351734.pdf ; UNCTAD Investment Policy Monitor (measure 3300), https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/investment-policy-monitor/measures/3300/india-fdi-policy-clarification-on-food-product-retail-trading ; Law.asia, "Decoding the FDI policy liberalization in food retail," https://law.asia/decoding-the-fdi-policy-liberalization-in-food-retail/

### 2.6 E-commerce — marketplace allowed, inventory prohibited; Press Note 2 of 2018

- **Baseline:** FDI **up to 100% under the automatic route is allowed in the *marketplace* model** of e-commerce; **FDI in the *inventory-based* model (B2C) is prohibited.** (VERIFIED FACT.)
- **Press Note 2 (2018 Series), DPIIT, dated 26 December 2018, effective 1 February 2019** (VERIFIED FACT):
  - A marketplace entity **shall not exercise ownership or control over the inventory** it sells;
  - A seller's inventory is **deemed controlled** by the marketplace (thus converting it to a prohibited inventory model) **if that seller purchases >25% of its goods from the marketplace entity or its group companies**;
  - No marketplace e-commerce entity may **mandate exclusivity** of any seller's products on its platform;
  - Marketplace entities must provide **services to all vendors on a fair and non-discriminatory basis**;
  - Compliance certificate (statutory auditor) due each year by 30 September; first truncated reporting for 1 Feb–31 Mar 2019.
- **Significance:** PN2/2018 directly reshaped Walmart-Flipkart and Amazon India structures (forced restructuring of preferred-seller relationships) and is the boundary that keeps foreign e-commerce capital in *marketplace* (not direct inventory) form. Food-product retail e-commerce of Indian-made food is the carve-out that sits *outside* this prohibition (see §2.5).
- **Sources:** DPIIT, Press Note 2 (2018 Series) PDF, https://dpiit.gov.in/sites/default/files/pn2_2018.pdf ; Khaitan & Co note, https://www.khaitanco.com/thought-leadership/FDI-in-E-commerce-activities-press-note-no-2 ; PIB clarification re PN2/2018, https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=187156 ; Ikigai Law, "Development of the FDI policy for the e-commerce sector," https://www.ikigailaw.com/article/383/development-of-the-fdi-policy-for-the-e-commerce-sector

## 3. Price-control track — DPCO 2013, NPPA, device caps, trade-margin rationalization

### 3.1 Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013 (DPCO 2013)

- **Instrument / date:** **S.O. 1221(E), dated 15 May 2013**, effective 15 May 2013, issued under the Essential Commodities Act, 1955. Administered by the **National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA)** (constituted 1997). (VERIFIED FACT.)
- **Scope:** Ceiling prices fixed for **scheduled formulations** = drugs in the **National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM)**, which forms Schedule-I to DPCO 2013. (VERIFIED FACT.)
- **Methodology — the key shift:** Moved from **cost-based** (DPCO 1995) to **market-based** pricing. The ceiling price = **simple average of the prices to retailer of all brands of that drug formulation having a market share of ≥1%**, plus a notified retailer margin (16%). (VERIFIED FACT.)
- **Non-scheduled drugs (Para 20):** Manufacturers of non-scheduled formulations **may not increase MRP by more than 10% in any 12 months**. (VERIFIED FACT.)
- **Annual revision (WPI):** Ceiling prices of scheduled drugs are revised annually using the **Wholesale Price Index (all commodities)** for the preceding calendar year, notified on/before **1 April** each year. (VERIFIED FACT.)
- **Para 19 — extraordinary powers:** NPPA "may, in extraordinary circumstances, if it considers necessary so to do in public interest, fix the ceiling price or retail price of any Drug for such period as it may deem fit" — the hook later used for **stents, knee implants, and trade-margin rationalization** of non-scheduled products. (VERIFIED FACT.)
- **NLEM history:** NLEM 2011 → **NLEM 2015** (376 medicines) → **NLEM 2022** (launched **13 September 2022**, **384 medicines**, 27 therapeutic categories; +34 added, −26 deleted). Inclusion in NLEM = automatic price regulation. (VERIFIED FACT.)

### 3.2 Coronary stents — price cap (2017)

- **Pre-condition:** Coronary stents added to **NLEM in July 2016** by the Health Ministry, bringing them within DPCO/NPPA jurisdiction. (VERIFIED FACT.)
- **Instrument / date:** **NPPA notification S.O. 412(E), dated 13 February 2017**, under **Para 19 (extraordinary powers)** of DPCO 2013, fixing ceiling prices for one year. (VERIFIED FACT.)
- **Caps (inclusive of all taxes):** **Bare-metal stents (BMS): INR 7,260**; **Drug-eluting stents (DES) / bioresorbable: INR ~29,600** (later sub-categorized). Represented price cuts of **up to ~85%** vs prevailing market prices (DES had been ~INR 1.21 lakh; BMS ~INR 45,000). (VERIFIED FACT for the caps; the "~85%" and prior prices are NPPA-stated / press-reported.)
- **Subsequent:** Revised annually per WPI — e.g., a **~4.2% upward revision in April 2019**. Caps remain in force and were reaffirmed in later years. (VERIFIED FACT.)
- **Foreign-investor relevance:** The cap directly compressed margins of foreign device majors (Abbott, Boston Scientific, Medtronic) and is repeatedly cited in the "exit threat" narrative (Abbott sought to withdraw Absorb/Xience premium stents post-cap). Conversely it improved the relative position of domestic/PE-backed makers (SMT, Meril). (STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION; cross-ref `medtech_pharmacy_distribution.md`.)
- **Sources:** Mondaq, "NPPA Revises Coronary Stent Price After A Year Of Price Cap" (S.O. 412(E), 13 Feb 2017, Para 19), https://www.mondaq.com/india/food-and-drugs-law/682908/nppa-revises-coronary-stent-price-after-a-year-of-price-cap ; AHA Circulation, "India and the Coronary Stent Market," https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circulationaha.117.028191 ; ORF, "Price-cap is after all just a new price: the case of cardiac stents in India," https://www.orfonline.org/research/price-cap-is-after-all-just-a-new-price-the-case-of-cardiac-stents-in-india ; Centre for Civil Society, "Price Cap Policy on Coronary Stents and its Unintended Consequences," https://ccs.in/price-cap-policy-coronary-stents-and-its-unintended-consequences ; Business Standard (4.2% hike, Apr 2019), https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/nppa-approves-hike-in-prices-of-coronary-stents-119040100608_1.html

### 3.3 Knee implants — price cap (2017)

- **Instrument / date:** **NPPA notification S.O. 2668(E), dated 16 August 2017**, under **Para 19** of DPCO 2013. (VERIFIED FACT.)
- **Caps & cuts:** Most-common cobalt-chromium total knee implant capped at **~INR 54,720** (+ taxes), **~65% lower** than the prior ~INR 1,58,324; titanium/oxidised-zirconium implants cut **~69%**; revision implants cut **~59%**. ### 3.4 Trade-margin rationalization (TMR)

A distinct mechanism from ceiling pricing: instead of fixing a price for a scheduled drug, NPPA caps the **trade margin** (distributor-to-retailer-to-patient mark-up) on *non-scheduled* products, using Para 19.

- **Anti-cancer (oncology) pilot — February 2019 (VERIFIED FACT):** NPPA capped the **trade margin at 30%** (measured at the first point of sale, typically price-to-distributor) on **42 selected non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs** (~500 brands). Result: ~50% average price reduction on affected brands; NPPA-estimated **annual savings ~INR 984 crore**. This was explicitly framed as a "proof of concept" for broader TMR.
  - Sources: PIB, "NPPA plays crucial role in making Cancer Drugs affordable," https://pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1670707 ; Business Standard, "42 more cancer drugs under price control, profit margin cap at 30%" (27 Feb 2019), https://www.business-standard.com/article/companies/42-more-cancer-drugs-under-price-control-profit-margin-cap-at-30-119022701252_1.html
- **COVID-era medical devices — 2021 (VERIFIED FACT):** TMR applied to devices via Para 19:
  - **Oxygen concentrators** — Order dated **3 June 2021**, trade margin capped at **70%**;
  - **Pulse oximeters, BP monitors, nebulizers, digital thermometers, glucometers** — Order dated **13 July 2021**, trade margin capped at **70%**; timeline subsequently extended (to 31 December 2022 and beyond).
  - Sources: Legal500, "NPPA Caps Trade Margin for Six Essential Medical Devices," https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/nppa-caps-trade-margin-for-six-essential-medical-devices/ ; Chambers, https://chambers.com/articles/nppa-caps-trade-margin-for-six-essential-medical-devices ; The Health Master (extension to 31 Dec 2022), https://thehealthmaster.com/2022/08/03/nppa-extends-timeline-for-trade-margin-capping-of-these-5-medical-devices/
- **Status of broader TMR rollout:** A general extension of TMR across all non-scheduled drugs has been discussed since 2019 but **not implemented economy-wide** in sources reviewed (remains pilot-plus-devices). (STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION / partial gap — see §6.)

### 3.5 Other recent device price actions

- NPPA continues annual WPI-linked ceiling-price revisions for NLEM drugs (e.g., ~138-890 formulations revised effective 1 April in recent years). (VERIFIED FACT — NPPA/PIB notifications.)
- Medical-device price monitoring continues; knee-implant caps extended to **15 Nov 2026**; periodic reviews of cementless knee implants, intraocular lenses (no separate ceiling found for IOLs beyond general DPCO/state pricing). (VERIFIED FACT / partial gap.)
- Sources: PIB, NPPA ceiling-price notifications, https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2100720 ; Medical Dialogues / The Health Master (above).

## 5. Market-structure / concentration notes (regulatory-perimeter angle)

These are not deal-census findings (covered in sibling files) but regulatory facts that bear on concentration:

- **Stents on NLEM (Jul 2016) + price cap (Feb 2017):** By regulatory design, the cap applies *equally* to all sellers, which (combined with 100%-automatic device FDI since 2015) shaped a market where foreign device majors' premium-product margins were compressed while domestic/PE-backed makers (SMT ~31% DES share per press, Meril) gained relative ground. (STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION — the regulatory facts are verified; the competitive-effect read is interpretation, cross-ref `medtech_pharmacy_distribution.md`.)
- **DPCO market-based methodology (simple average of ≥1%-share brands):** Because the ceiling is a market average, **highly concentrated** molecules (few large brands) can yield *higher* ceilings than fragmented ones — a structural feature relevant to whether PE-led roll-ups of branded generics raise or lower regulated ceilings. (EXPERT INTERPRETATION.)
- **MBRT 51% + e-commerce prohibition vs food-retail 100% + e-commerce allowed:** This asymmetry channelled foreign retail/quick-commerce capital toward (a) the *marketplace* e-commerce route (Amazon, Flipkart/Walmart) and (b) the *Indian-made-food* 100% route, rather than into 51% multi-brand brick-and-mortar. Directly relevant to the quick-commerce/food-delivery share work. (STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION; cross-ref `quickcommerce_delivery_shares.md`, `packaged_food_pe_census.md`.)
- **Insurance to 100% (2025/26):** Removes the last hard FDI ceiling among the financial-adjacent health sectors (health insurance/TPAs); relevant to the insurance/TPA arm. (VERIFIED FACT; cross-ref `insurance_tpa_review.csv`.)
