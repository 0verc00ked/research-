## 1. Overview

This cell maps foreign private-equity, sovereign-wealth, pension, family-office and strategic-corporate (MNC) capital across India's packaged food, FMCG, beverages and direct-to-consumer (D2C) food sector. Three structural patterns emerge from the evidence:

1. **Belgian and Singaporean conduits dominate the "better-for-you" / dairy-snacking and condiments cluster.** Verlinvest (Belgium, the De Spoelberch family office behind AB InBev wealth) is the single most recurrent foreign sponsor across Indian packaged food D2C — Veeba, Epigamia/Drums Food, and (covered elsewhere) Sresta's earlier era and others. Temasek (Singapore SWF) and Singapore-domiciled PE/VC vehicles (Venturi Partners, Gateway Partners, NewQuest) recur on the dairy/protein-D2C side (Country Delight, Licious) and the bakery side (Mrs Bectors).

2. **Strategic MNCs hold outright control of the most profitable, highest-volume categories** — Mondelez (US) controls ~64% of Indian chocolate via Cadbury; PepsiCo's franchise bottling is consolidated under Varun Beverages (Indian promoter RJ Corp but with ~19.5% foreign FPI float); Orkla (Norway) controls MTR and is now consolidating Eastern Condiments + a planned IPO; Hershey (US) operates a wholly-owned subsidiary after exiting its Godrej JV. These are control positions, not minority PE stakes.

3. **The exit/IPO cycle is now mature.** Lighthouse and IIFL part-exited Bikaji at its Nov-2022 IPO; CX Partners + Gateway Partners (Singapore) part-exited Mrs Bectors at its Dec-2020 IPO; Helion exited iD Fresh at 10x; ITC and Tata Consumer have become the domestic consolidators buying foreign-PE-backed brands (Sresta/24 Mantra, Organic India, Capital Foods, Soulfull). Conversely, the craft-beer thesis has soured — Kirin Holdings (Japan) is exploring a written-down exit from Bira 91/B9 Beverages.

**Nutrition-category lens (UPF/HFSS vs. healthy):** The bulk of foreign capital here funds ultra-processed / HFSS (high fat-salt-sugar) categories — namkeen/bhujia (Bikaji), biscuits (Mrs Bectors), condiments/sauces (Veeba), chocolate (Mondelez, Hershey), sugar-sweetened beverages (Varun/PepsiCo, Bira beer), frozen snacks/fries (McCain), QSR momos (Wow! Momo). A smaller, "halo" tranche funds nominally healthier categories — organic staples (Sresta/24 Mantra, Organic India), fresh dairy (Country Delight, Milk Mantra, Akshayakalpa, Epigamia Greek yogurt), and fresh meat (Licious) — though several of these (sweetened yogurts, ready-to-cook) blur the line. This category skew is itself a finding: foreign institutional capital has disproportionately scaled HFSS/UPF distribution in India.

## 2. Detailed findings narrative

### 2.1 Bikaji Foods International (snacks / namkeen / bhujia — UPF)

- **Lighthouse Funds** (India-focused PE; partners include foreign LPs) acquired **12.5% for ₹90 crore in April 2014**, at an implied valuation of ~₹720 crore. [STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION — Lighthouse own release] (https://www.lhfunds.com/news/iifl-fund-acquires-stake-in-bikaji-foods/)
- **IIFL Special Opportunities Fund** (managed by IIFL AMC) invested **₹251 crore (announced 18 May 2018)** via a mix of primary and secondary, taking a minority stake (press cites ~7.4% pre-IPO). [STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION — Lighthouse release + press] (https://www.lhfunds.com/news/iifl-fund-acquires-stake-in-bikaji-foods/)
- **IPO (Nov 2022):** Bikaji listed via a **100% offer-for-sale of 2,93,73,984 shares aggregating ₹880.86 crore at ₹300/share**; opened 3 Nov 2022, closed 7 Nov 2022, listed 16 Nov 2022 on BSE/NSE. Lighthouse contributed ~40% of the OFS (halving a ~10% holding) and IIFL ~37% (trimming a 7.4% stake toward ~3%). [VERIFIED FACT for IPO size/price; press-reported for the OFS split] (https://www.chittorgarh.com/ipo/bikaji-foods-ipo/1332/)
- **Foreign angle:** Lighthouse and IIFL are India-domiciled managers; foreign capital here is indirect (LPs). Not a direct foreign-control case, but included for the institutional-capital census. *Original Lighthouse 12.5% stake reportedly returned 10x+ on part-exit* [press-reported] (https://startupstorymedia.com/insights-lighthouse-exits-bikaji-foods-partially-with-10x-returns/).

### 2.2 Bira 91 / B9 Beverages (craft beer — alcohol/UPF)

- **Sofina (Belgium, family investment company):** led a **$50M round in 2018**; participated again in an **April 2020 ~$20M Pre-Series C bridge** alongside Sequoia Capital India. [STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION — press, multiple] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bira_91)
- **Kirin Holdings (Japan, listed integrated-beverages strategic):** first India investment — **$30M in January 2021** for an under-10% stake, a mix of equity and debt. [STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION — Inc42] (https://inc42.com/buzz/bira-91-raises-30-mn-from-japans-kirin-holdings-for-under-10-stake/)
- **Kirin follow-on:** additional **$25M (announced ~2024, structured partly as ECB/Series D)**, lifting Kirin beyond ~20%; an unnamed US investor was reported alongside, targeting ~$50M total at a ~$600M pre-money. [press-reported] (https://www.entrepreneur.com/en-in/news-and-trends/bira-91-secures-usd-25-mn-in-series-d-from-kirin-holdings/475832)
- **Largest shareholder:** Kirin held **20.1% as of June 2025**, ahead of founder Ankur Jain (~17.8%). [press-reported, citing shareholding data] (https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/bira-91s-japanese-investor-kirin-eyes-exit-amid-deepening-losses-employee-complaints)
- **Distress / planned exit (Nov 2025):** B9 reported an **FY24 net loss of ₹748 crore, sales down 22% to ₹638 crore, debt ~₹1,000 crore**; Kirin has **written down its debt and equity** and engaged EY to manage an exit by selling its debt in two tranches. [press-reported — Business Standard / Outlook Business] (https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/japan-kirin-holdings-exit-b9-beverages-bira-91-financial-crisis-debt-loss-125111900116_1.html)
- Total raised across ~21 rounds: **~$450–457M** [press-reported, Tracxn/Dealroom] (https://app.dealroom.co/companies/bira91). Other backers: Peak XV (ex-Sequoia India), MUFG Bank.
