## 1. Overview

This dossier documents the *tax and structuring plumbing* — not the individual deals per se, but the legal architecture that determines **how foreign private-equity, sovereign-wealth, pension and institutional capital enters Indian healthcare/pharma/food/agri, where the holding vehicles sit, and how the gains escape or minimise Indian tax.** The other research cells catalogue the deals; this cell explains *why the money flows through Mauritius, Singapore, the Netherlands, Cayman, and increasingly GIFT City IFSC*, and what changed in the 2016–2025 period.

**The arc of the story (evidence-graded below):**

1. **Pre-2017:** India's treaties with Mauritius and Singapore exempted capital gains on Indian shares from Indian tax (residence-based taxation). Since neither jurisdiction taxes such gains, the effective rate on exit was **zero**. ~34% of cumulative FDI (2000–2017) came via Mauritius; ~16% via Singapore. *(VERIFIED FACT — see §2, §9.)*
2. **2016 protocols:** India amended both treaties to **source-based** capital-gains taxation effective **1 April 2017**, with **grandfathering** for shares acquired before that date and a 2017–2019 transition at 50% of the domestic rate. *(VERIFIED FACT — primary CBDT/Gazette notification.)*
3. **GAAR (1 April 2017) + MLI/PPT (FY2020-21):** General anti-avoidance and the Principal Purpose Test layered substance requirements on top of treaty access. *(VERIFIED FACT.)*
4. **Result:** PE routing shifted **from Mauritius toward Singapore** (Singapore overtook Mauritius as top FDI source by FY2018-19), and a third pole emerged — **GIFT City IFSC**, an onshore "deemed-foreign" jurisdiction offering a 10-year tax holiday (Section 80LA), tax-neutral fund relocation (Sections 47(viiac)/(viiad)), and Category-III AIF exemptions (Section 10(4D)). *(VERIFIED FACT / STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION.)*
5. **Finance (No.2) Act 2024:** Recast the capital-gains regime — **LTCG 12.5%, STCG 20%, indexation removed**, and a new **buyback-as-deemed-dividend** regime from 1 Oct 2024. These apply to non-residents too, though treaty relief and grandfathering survive. *(VERIFIED FACT.)*

**Why this matters for the investigation:** The structuring layer is *how* concentration of control over hospitals, pharma and food supply by foreign capital is achieved at near-zero exit tax, often through opaque offshore SPVs whose ultimate beneficial owners are only disclosed in CCI merger orders and SEBI BO filings. The architecture is **legal**; the public-interest concerns are (a) erosion of India's tax base on the appreciation of strategic Indian assets, (b) opacity of ultimate beneficial ownership, and (c) round-tripping risk (Indian money returning disguised as FDI).

## 2. India–Mauritius DTAA and the 2016 Protocol

### 2.1 The pre-2017 regime (the "Mauritius route")

Under the original India–Mauritius Double Taxation Avoidance Convention, **capital gains on alienation of shares of Indian companies by a Mauritius resident were taxable only in Mauritius** (residence-based taxation, Article 13). Because Mauritius levies no capital-gains tax, the effective Indian tax on exit was **zero**. This made Mauritius the dominant conduit for foreign investment into India — approximately **one-third of all FDI into India** was structured through Mauritius pre-2017.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Sources: https://bcajonline.org/journal/protocol-to-india-mauritius-tax-treaty-2016-an-analysis-2/ ; https://www.klgates.com/Investing-in-India--Take-a-Look-at-the-Amended-India-Mauritius-Tax-Treaty-Before-You-Do-05-18-2016

### 2.2 The 2016 Protocol — the pivot to source-based taxation

The Protocol amending the India–Mauritius Convention was **signed on 10 May 2016 at Port Louis, Mauritius**; it **entered into force in India on 19 July 2016** and was **notified in the Official Gazette on 11 August 2016** (CBDT Notification No. 68/2016).
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT** (primary — CBDT notification)
- Sources: https://incometaxindia.gov.in/communications/notification/notification682016.pdf ; https://incometaxindia.gov.in/Lists/Press%20Releases/Attachments/468/Press-release-Indo-Mauritius-10-05-2016.pdf (note: PIB/incometaxindia PDFs returned HTTP 403 to the automated fetcher; content corroborated via BCAJ, K&L Gates, IndiaCorpLaw, EY analyses)

**Core changes:**
- **Source-based capital-gains taxation** for shares of Indian companies **acquired on or after 1 April 2017** — India (the source country) gets the right to tax. *(VERIFIED FACT)*
- **Grandfathering:** Gains on shares **acquired before 1 April 2017** remain taxable only in Mauritius (i.e., effectively exempt in India), **regardless of when sold**. *(VERIFIED FACT)*
- **Transition period 1 April 2017 – 31 March 2019:** Indian tax limited to **50% of the domestic rate**, subject to a **Limitation of Benefits (LOB)** test. From 1 April 2019, full domestic rates apply. *(VERIFIED FACT)*
- **LOB:** Treaty benefit denied to shell/conduit entities; a resident is deemed a shell if its total expenditure on operations in Mauritius is **less than Mauritian Rs 1.5 million (≈ INR 2.7 million)** in the 12 months preceding the gain. *(STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION — widely reported LOB threshold; exact figure should be verified against the Gazette text.)*
- Sources: https://indiacorplaw.in/2016/07/13/india-mauritius-dtaa-protocol-analyzing/ ; https://www.klgates.com/Investing-in-India--Take-a-Look-at-the-Amended-India-Mauritius-Tax-Treaty-Before-You-Do-05-18-2016 ; https://www.mondaq.com/india/tax-authorities/1774750/article-134-taxation-of-capital-gains-on-shares-in-indian-companies-an-analysis-of-the-grandfathering-clause-and-its-implications

**Note on withholding for debt:** Mauritius retained a competitive **7.5% interest withholding** rate (vs Singapore up to 15%, Cyprus 10%), keeping Mauritius attractive for *debt* routing even after the equity capital-gains advantage was removed.
- Classification: **STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION (press/advisory-reported)**
- Source: https://ksandk.com/tax/india-singapore-dtaa-capital-gains-grandfathering/

### 2.3 The 2024 Protocol — Principal Purpose Test added to Mauritius treaty

A further **Protocol was signed on 7 March 2024** to incorporate **BEPS minimum standards**: an amended **Preamble** and a **Principal Purpose Test (PPT)**. The PPT denies treaty benefits where **"one of the principal purposes"** of an arrangement is to obtain the benefit.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Source: https://www.ey.com/en_in/technical/alerts-hub/2024/04/protocol-to-india-mauritius-dtaa-signed-to-include-principal-purpose-test

**CBDT Circular No. 01/2025 (21 January 2025)** clarified:
- The PPT applies **prospectively** from the date the amending protocol enters into force.
- **Grandfathered capital gains on pre-April-2017 shares remain outside the PPT** and continue to be governed by the specific DTAA terms.
- Grandfathering protection is **limited to capital gains on shares** — *not* to dividends, debenture gains, or indirect transfers, which remain subject to the PPT prospectively.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Sources: https://www.pwc.com/mu/en/services/tax/Taxtimes/india-treaty-ppt.html ; https://www.apexgroup.com/insights/cbdt-clarifies-principal-purpose-test-under-the-mauritius-india-tax-treaty/ ; http://nishithdesai.com/NewsDetails/15244

## 3. India–Singapore DTAA and the 2016 (Third Protocol) Amendment

### 3.1 The pre-2017 Singapore advantage

The India–Singapore DTAA (originally signed **24 January 1994**) mirrored the Mauritius treaty: **capital gains on Indian shares held by Singapore residents were taxable only in Singapore**, and since Singapore does not tax such gains, the effective Indian rate was **zero**. This made Singapore a favoured PE/holding domicile, with the *added* benefit (vs Mauritius) of genuine commercial substance, a robust legal system, and an existing LOB clause.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Source: https://www.corporateservices.com/singapore/guides/tax/india-singapore-dtaa-guide/

### 3.2 The Third Protocol (December 2016)

The **Third Protocol, signed December 2016, effective 1 April 2017**, aligned the Singapore treaty with the amended Mauritius treaty:
- **Source-based taxation** of capital gains on Indian shares acquired **on/after 1 April 2017**.
- **Grandfathering** for shares acquired **before 1 April 2017** (continue taxable only in Singapore).
- **Transition 1 April 2017 – 31 March 2019:** 50% of the domestic rate, subject to LOB.
- **LOB (Article 24A):** A Singapore resident **cannot claim** capital-gains treaty benefits if its **annual operational expenditure in Singapore is less than SGD 200,000 (≈ INR 5,000,000)** in each 12-month period within the 24 months preceding the gain, or if the **primary purpose** of the Singapore entity was to obtain treaty benefits.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Sources: https://ksandk.com/tax/india-singapore-dtaa-capital-gains-grandfathering/ ; https://www.majmudarindia.com/the-india-singapore-tax-treaty-has-been-amended/ ; https://tax.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2017/01/india-singapore-dtaa-meets-fate-mauritius-cyprus/

## 5. GAAR, MLI / Principal Purpose Test, and Indirect-Transfer Tax

### 5.1 GAAR (General Anti-Avoidance Rules)

GAAR was legislated (Chapter X-A of the Income-tax Act, 1961) and made **effective 1 April 2017** (FY2017-18 onward). It empowers tax authorities to deny tax benefits from an **"impermissible avoidance arrangement"** whose **main purpose** is a tax benefit and which has a "tainted element" (lacks commercial substance, etc.). GAAR applies to **both domestic and cross-border** transactions.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Sources: https://taxguru.in/income-tax/gaar-ppt-beginning-era-co-existence.html ; https://www.business-standard.com/article/economy-policy/invoke-either-gaar-or-principal-purpose-test-asifma-tells-centre-118022300828_1.html

### 5.2 MLI and the Principal Purpose Test (PPT)

India signed the **OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI)**; it entered into force for India in 2019, with provisions effective **from 1 April 2020** for covered treaties. India adopted the **PPT**, which denies treaty benefits if **"one of the principal purposes"** of an arrangement was to obtain the benefit — a **broader, lower threshold** than GAAR's "main purpose," and applicable only to **cross-border treaty-benefit** claims.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Sources: https://database.taxsutra.com/articles/52f2030bd09bc2a1c5809156e7ef66/expert_article ; https://carajput.com/blog/principal-purpose-test-ppt-under-indias-dtaa/

**GAAR vs PPT coexistence:** Both can apply; industry (ASIFMA) urged the government to invoke *either* GAAR *or* PPT, not both, on a given arrangement. CBDT Circular 01/2025 confirmed grandfathered Mauritius/Singapore/Cyprus capital gains remain outside the PPT.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Source: https://www.business-standard.com/article/economy-policy/invoke-either-gaar-or-principal-purpose-test-asifma-tells-centre-118022300828_1.html

### 5.3 Indirect-transfer tax (the "Vodafone" provision) — relevant to offshore PE exits

**Explanation 5 to Section 9(1)(i)** of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (inserted by **Finance Act 2012**, retrospectively after the Supreme Court's *Vodafone International Holdings BV v. Union of India* (2012) 341 ITR 1 ruling) **deems shares of a foreign company to be Indian-situated assets** if they derive substantial value from Indian assets — taxing **offshore share transfers** of foreign holding companies that indirectly own Indian businesses.
- **Substantial-value test:** Indian assets FMV **> INR 10 crore** *and* **≥ 50%** of the foreign entity's total assets.
- **Vodafone deal facts:** May 2007, Vodafone International Holdings BV (**Netherlands**) acquired ~67% indirect stake in Hutchison Essar via shares of a **Cayman Islands** entity (CGP Investments) for **USD 11.1 billion**. SC held untaxable absent a look-through provision; Parliament responded retrospectively.
- **Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2021** withdrew the retrospective application for pre-28 May 2012 transactions (settling Vodafone and Cairn), but the **prospective indirect-transfer regime remains** — a live risk for PE exits structured as sales of offshore holdcos owning Indian healthcare/pharma/food assets.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT** (statute + SC judgment); **EXPERT INTERPRETATION** (relevance to PE exit structuring)
- Sources: https://www.nishithdesai.com/Content/document/pdf/Articles/171023_A_Indirect-Transfer-Taxation-in-India.pdf ; https://beaconfiling.com/glossary/indirect-transfer ; https://taxguru.in/income-tax/taxation-offshore-indirect-transfers-indian-capital-assets.html

## 6. GIFT City IFSC — the Onshore-Offshore Fund Hub

GIFT City (Gujarat International Finance Tec-City) International Financial Services Centre, inaugurated as an IFSC in 2015 and regulated since 2020 by the **IFSCA** (unified regulator), is a **"deemed foreign jurisdiction"**; units set up there are treated as **non-residents under FEMA**. Funds operate under the **IFSCA (Fund Management) Regulations, 2022**.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Sources: https://gift.treelife.in/private-equity-venture-capital-in-gift-city/ ; https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/CSJ/July-2024/22.pdf

### 6.1 The tax-holiday and fund incentives

- **Section 80LA:** 100% tax holiday on business income for **any 10 consecutive years out of 15** for IFSC units (incl. fund-management entities). *(VERIFIED FACT)*
- **Category I & II AIFs:** Tax **pass-through** under Section 115UB (taxed in investors' hands). **Category III AIFs** are generally taxed at fund level — except IFSC Specified Cat-III AIFs (see below). *(VERIFIED FACT)*
- **Section 10(4D):** Exemption for a **"Specified Fund"** (Cat-III IFSC AIF, all units held by non-residents other than sponsor/manager) on income from transfer of securities **(except shares of an Indian company)**, securities issued by non-residents, and securitisation-trust income. *(VERIFIED FACT)*
- **Section 115AD (as amended):** **10%** rate on dividend/interest income of a Specified Fund, to the extent attributable to non-resident units. **Section 196D:** no withholding on income exempt under 10(4D). *(VERIFIED FACT)*
- **Category III AIF — zero capital-gains tax** on transfer of *specified securities* for non-resident investors, subject to conditions. *(STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION — advisory-reported; conditions material.)*
- Sources: https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/tax-benefits-for-category-iii-aifs-located-in-ifscs/ ; https://natlawreview.com/article/india-incentivising-category-iii-aifs-ifsc ; https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2024-02/CTC%20Journal%20-%20Article%20on%20Taxation%20of%20IFSC%20Funds%20and%20its%20Investors.pdf ; https://cleartax.in/s/section-115ad-of-income-tax-act

### 6.2 Tax-neutral relocation of offshore funds (the explicit "move from Singapore/Mauritius" lever)

**Finance Act, 2021** inserted **Sections 47(viiac) and 47(viiad)** providing **capital-gains exemption** on the **tax-neutral relocation** of an **"original fund"** (offshore — e.g., Mauritius, Singapore, Delaware) to a **"resultant fund"** (IFSC AIF), executed via a unit-swap. Both the fund and its investors are shielded from tax on the migration.
- **Sunset clause extended from 31 March 2025 to 31 March 2030.**
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Sources: https://natlawreview.com/article/relocation-offshore-funds-to-gift-city ; https://www.nishithdesai.com/NewsDetails/4822 ; https://ifsca.gov.in/Document/10_EY-GIFT_tax_benefits_050225.pdf

### 6.3 Retail/NRI access and growth metrics

- **June 2024:** SEBI/IFSCA permitted **100% NRI ownership** of GIFT-IFSC funds (removing the prior 50% cap), and opened relocation of foreign funds (set up as FPIs) into IFSC **Category-III AIFs**.
- **From April 2026:** Mutual funds/ETFs can relocate to GIFT City **without capital-gains tax** — explicitly to draw bases away from Singapore/Mauritius.
- **Growth:** End-2024 — **139 Fund Management Entities (FMEs)**, **>USD 14.88bn** total commitments. By Q3 FY26 — **194 FMEs, 310 active schemes, >USD 32bn** aggregate commitments.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT** (regulatory changes); **STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION** (growth figures — IFSCA/press-reported)
- Sources: https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/226923/indians-get-new-route-worlds-markets-gift-city/ ; https://gift.treelife.in/private-equity-venture-capital-in-gift-city/ ; https://api.giftgujarat.in/public/downloads/ifsc/GiftCity-FundsBooklet.pdf

## 7. The Capital-Gains Regime for Non-Residents — Finance (No.2) Act 2024

The **Finance (No.2) Act, 2024 (enacted 16/20 August 2024)** overhauled the capital-gains regime. CBDT issued clarifying FAQs (PIB PRID 2036604).

| Item | Pre-23 July 2024 | From 23 July 2024 | Applies to non-residents? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LTCG (listed equity, STT-paid, 112A)** | 10% above INR 1 lakh | **12.5%** above **INR 1.25 lakh** | Yes |
| **LTCG (other assets, incl. unlisted/foreign)** | 20% with indexation | **12.5% without indexation** | Yes |
| **STCG (listed equity, 111A)** | 15% | **20%** | Yes |
| **Indexation benefit** | Available | **Removed** (most assets) | — |
| **Resident-only relief (lower of new vs old)** | n/a | Available to **resident individuals/HUF only** | **No — NRIs/non-residents excluded** |

- The new capital-gains provisions **come into force from 23.7.2024** and apply to any transfer on/after that date.
- A uniform **12.5% LTCG** applies regardless of listed/unlisted, STT-paid or not, resident or non-resident (with carve-outs).
- The grandfathering/indexation relief for property bought before 23 July 2024 is **available only to resident individuals and HUFs** — **NRIs and other non-residents cannot use it**.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Sources: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2036604&reg=3&lang=2 (CBDT FAQs — PIB blocked automated fetch; corroborated below) ; https://www.bdo.global/en-gb/insights/tax/world-wide-tax/india-budget-2024-25-measures-affecting-nonresident-investors ; https://cleartax.in/s/long-term-capital-gains-ltcg-tax ; https://www.grantthornton.in/globalassets/1.-member-firms/india/assets/pdfs/alerts/gt_tax_alert_amendments_to_fb_2024_august_16_2024.pdf

### 7.1 Surcharge / FPI effective rate (Section 115AD)

- FPIs are taxed under **Section 115AD**: LTCG and STCG on securities at the above rates, **plus surcharge and 4% health-and-education cess**.
- Surcharge on **dividend income and 115AD(1)(b) capital gains is capped at 15%** (i.e., the higher 25%/37% slabs do not apply to these income streams).
- **Treaty override:** under each DTAA, the more beneficial of treaty rate vs domestic rate applies, subject to **Tax Residency Certificate (TRC)** and now GAAR/PPT. A Singapore-resident FPI on listed LTCG may apply a **10%** treaty rate where beneficial.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT** (115AD/surcharge cap); **STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION** (treaty-rate application — fact-specific)
- Sources: https://cleartax.in/s/section-115ad-of-income-tax-act ; https://www.nseindia.com/static/invest/first-time-investor-income-tax-for-foreign-investors ; https://www.in.kpmg.com/TaxFlashNews-INT/Foreign-Portfolio-Investors-Tax-Rate-Card.pdf

### 7.2 Buyback tax — shifted to shareholders from 1 October 2024

- The company-level **buyback tax under Section 115QA was repealed**; from **1 October 2024**, buyback consideration is a **deemed dividend in the shareholder's hands under new Section 2(22)(f)**, taxed at the shareholder's applicable rate.
- **No deduction** for cost of acquisition against the deemed-dividend portion (Section 57 deduction barred). The acquisition cost is treated as a **capital loss** (consideration deemed nil under Section 46A read with Section 68 Companies Act) that can be set off / carried forward up to 8 years.
- **Withholding:** 10% for residents; for **non-residents, "rates in force"** — **20% (plus surcharge/cess)** or as notified, subject to applicable treaty (dividend article) where GAAR/MLI conditions are met.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Sources: https://www.mondaq.com/india/shareholders/1511692/the-new-tax-landscape-for-buy-backs-implications-of-the-finance-no-2-act-2024 ; https://taxguru.in/income-tax/buyback-tax-shifted-shareholders-deemed-dividend-october-2024.html ; https://www.snrlaw.in/redefining-buy-back-tax-implications-under-finance-act-no-2-2024/

## 8. AIF and FPI Taxation (domestic + IFSC)

- **Category I & II AIFs:** **Pass-through** under **Section 115UB** — income (other than business income) taxed directly in investors' hands as if invested directly.
- **Category III AIFs (domestic):** Taxed at **fund level**; characterisation has been litigation-prone.
- **Category III IFSC AIFs (Specified Fund):** Enjoy Section 10(4D) exemption + 115AD 10% concessional rate (see §6.1), conditional on all non-sponsor/manager units being held by non-residents.
- **FPIs:** Taxed under Section 115AD (see §7.1); also subject to **SEBI FPI Regulations** and the BO-disclosure regime (see §10).
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Sources: https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/tax-benefits-for-category-iii-aifs-located-in-ifscs/ ; https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/aif-taxation-india ; https://cleartax.in/s/section-115ad-of-income-tax-act

## 9. Round-Tripping and Treaty-Shopping Concerns

- **Round-tripping:** Indian-origin funds routed abroad (notably Mauritius) and re-entering as "FDI" to obtain treaty benefits and conceal beneficial owners. The Government's own **2012 White Paper on Black Money (Ministry of Finance/Department of Revenue)** flagged that **Mauritius (41.80%) and Singapore (9.17%)** topped cumulative FDI inflows (April 2000–March 2011) and that such tiny economies "cannot be the sources of such huge investments," implying routing for tax avoidance and identity concealment, including by Indian residents investing in their own companies.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT** (the White Paper made these statements); **EXPERT INTERPRETATION / PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS** (the extent of actual round-tripping is inherently unverifiable)
- Source: https://dor.gov.in/sites/default/files/inline-documents/FinalBlackMoney.pdf
- **Participatory Notes (P-Notes):** Long flagged (incl. a 2007 IMF working paper) as enabling round-tripping; subsequently tightened by SEBI. *(STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION)*
- **Policy response:** GAAR (§5.1), treaty source-based taxation (§2–3), and the SEBI BO-disclosure regime (§10) are the principal counter-measures.
- Sources: https://thefederal.com/liberalisation30/how-black-money-came-back-legally-as-foreign-investment ; https://www.cbgaindia.org/blog/investing-india-mauritius/

## 10. SEBI FPI Beneficial-Ownership Disclosure (2023–2024)

Triggered by concerns over circumvention of minimum public shareholding and concealment of ultimate ownership (echoing the Adani-Hindenburg episode), SEBI overhauled FPI BO disclosure:

- **31 May 2023:** Consultation paper proposing enhanced disclosure for **"High-Risk FPIs"**; pension funds and widely-held public retail funds classed **"Moderate Risk"** and exempt.
- **24 August 2023 Circular:** FPIs meeting prescribed criteria must disclose **all entities with any ownership, economic interest, or control on a full look-through basis, up to all natural persons, with no de-minimis threshold.**
- **Criteria (the high-risk triggers):** (i) FPIs holding **>50% of their Indian equity AUM in a single corporate group**; or (ii) FPIs with **Indian equity AUM > INR 25,000 crore** (later raised to **INR 50,000 crore**).
- **15 March 2024 Circular:** Additional exemption for certain FPIs (e.g., where the >50%-single-group holding is in a company with no identified promoter), narrowing the granular-BO net.
- Classification: **VERIFIED FACT**
- Sources: https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2024/05/new-sebi-fpi-beneficial-owner-disclosure-norms/ ; https://trilegal.com/knowledge_repository/sebi-circular-on-additional-disclosures-by-foreign-portfolio-investors/ ; https://indiacorplaw.in/2025/05/sebis-fpi-disclosure-shift-overhaul-viability-of-the-trade-off.html

**Investigation relevance:** This regime is the *primary public lever* to pierce offshore PE/FPI structures down to natural-person beneficial owners — directly bearing on whether foreign control of Indian healthcare/food assets can be transparently mapped.

## 13. Key Dates Timeline

- **2012** — Finance Act 2012: indirect-transfer Explanation 5 to s.9(1)(i) (retrospective, post-Vodafone).
- **10 May 2016** — India–Mauritius Protocol signed (Port Louis).
- **19 Jul 2016 / 11 Aug 2016** — Protocol into force / Gazette-notified (Notn 68/2016).
- **Dec 2016** — India–Singapore Third Protocol signed.
- **1 Apr 2017** — Source-based capital-gains taxation begins (Mauritius & Singapore); **GAAR effective**.
- **1 Apr 2017 – 31 Mar 2019** — Transition period (50% of domestic rate, subject to LOB).
- **1 Apr 2019** — Full domestic capital-gains rates on Mauritius/Singapore exits (post-cutoff shares).
- **1 Apr 2020** — MLI/PPT provisions effective for India's covered treaties.
- **2021** — Finance Act 2021: ss.47(viiac)/(viiad) tax-neutral GIFT-IFSC fund relocation; Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act 2021 withdraws retrospective indirect-transfer.
- **24 Aug 2023** — SEBI FPI granular BO-disclosure circular.
- **7 Mar 2024** — India–Mauritius Protocol adding PPT signed.
- **15 Mar 2024** — SEBI additional FPI BO exemption circular.
- **23 Jul 2024** — New capital-gains regime effective (LTCG 12.5%, STCG 20%, indexation removed).
- **16/20 Aug 2024** — Finance (No.2) Act 2024 enacted.
- **1 Oct 2024** — Buyback-as-deemed-dividend regime (s.2(22)(f)); s.115QA repealed.
- **21 Jan 2025** — CBDT Circular 01/2025 on PPT (grandfathering preserved for pre-2017 share capital gains).
- **31 Mar 2030** — Extended sunset for tax-neutral GIFT-IFSC fund relocation.
- **Apr 2026** — Tax-free relocation of mutual funds/ETFs to GIFT City begins.

## 14. Gaps / Unverified

1. **Primary regulator PDFs blocked to automated fetcher.** PIB (pib.gov.in) and incometaxindia.gov.in PDFs returned **HTTP 403/cert errors**; CCI PDFs returned cert errors. Their *contents* were corroborated via Big-4 / law-firm summaries and press, but the **exact statutory/Gazette wording was not machine-extracted here**. For a parliamentary-grade citation, the following should be opened manually: CBDT Notn 68/2016, the s.13 amended treaty text, CCI order C-2023/07/1044 (Quality Care), and CBDT Circular 01/2025.
2. **Exact LOB thresholds** in the Mauritius treaty (MUR 1.5m figure) and the precise transition-period mechanics should be confirmed against the Gazette text, not advisory summaries.
3. **Advent / Bharat Serums acquisition vehicle jurisdiction** — entities **Ansamira Ltd** and **Miransa Ltd** are confirmed as Advent fund affiliates, but their **incorporation jurisdiction (likely Mauritius/Cayman) is UNVERIFIED**; the original CCI order should be retrieved.
4. **Food/agri offshore-vehicle disclosure is thinner.** Confirmed Singapore vehicle for Temasek/Haldiram (Jongsong Investments Pte Ltd); most other food/agrifoodtech deals (PAG, etc.) in 2023-25 are reported without disclosed SPV jurisdiction. PAG's recent India deals (Pravesha, Manjushree Technopack) are pharma-packaging, **not dairy** — an earlier press conflation; verify before citing as "food/agri."
5. **Round-tripping magnitude is inherently unverifiable** — flagged by the Government's own 2012 White Paper as a concern, but the actual quantum of Indian-origin money re-entering as FDI is a **PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS**, not a measured fact.
6. **Deal values are largely press-reported.** Where CCI orders state stakes, those are VERIFIED; the *consideration* amounts (USD 800m for Care, ~USD 2bn for Manipal, USD 1bn for Haldiram) are **press-reported** and should not be cited as official unless confirmed in a filing.
7. **Surcharge interaction with treaty rates** for FPIs is fact-specific; the 15% surcharge cap on 115AD gains is confirmed, but effective blended rates per jurisdiction were not computed deal-by-deal here.
8. **The KPMG FPI rate-card and Grant Thornton FA2024 PDFs** were retrieved but were image/compressed and not text-extractable; rates were corroborated from CBDT FAQs / BDO / ClearTax instead.
