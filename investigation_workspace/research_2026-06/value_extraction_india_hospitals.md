# Value-Extraction Mechanisms & Indian Listed-Hospital Financials

**Research cell:** PE value-extraction playbook (globally evidenced) + India application; Indian listed-hospital financial metrics (ARPOB, EBITDA margin, occupancy, payer mix, leverage).
**Compiled:** 2026-06-24
**Scope note:** Focus on FOREIGN PE / sovereign-wealth / institutional capital and where Indian PE interacts with it. Every numeric claim carries an inline source URL and an evidence classification.

**Evidence classification key:** `VERIFIED FACT` (in a filing / regulator order / company release / audited rating report) | `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION` | `EXPERT INTERPRETATION` | `PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS` | `UNPROVEN CLAIM`. Press-reported figures are labelled "press-reported" and are not treated as official unless corroborated by a filing/order/release.

---

## 1. Executive overview

Two threads were investigated.

**Thread 1 — the PE value-extraction playbook and its India application.** The global playbook (debt-loading via leveraged buyouts; dividend recapitalizations; sale-leaseback of hospital real estate to REITs; management/monitoring fees; buy-and-build add-on roll-ups; price/ARPOB escalation) is heavily documented in the US/Europe in peer-reviewed and regulator-adjacent literature. **In India, the picture is materially different and must not be over-claimed.** The most aggressive extraction tools (US-style REIT sale-leasebacks with rent-burdened operating companies; large debt-funded dividend recaps) are **largely ABSENT or only partially present** in Indian listed hospitals as of FY25. There is **no listed dedicated healthcare REIT in India**, and SEBI/Companies Act/RBI prudential limits constrain dividend recaps. The clearest India applications found are: (a) the **Fortis → Religare/RHT Health Trust** Singapore business-trust real-estate spin-off (2012) and its reversal (2019 buyback at Rs 4,650 crore) — the single closest Indian analogue to a sale-leaseback / asset-light real-estate monetization; (b) **buy-and-build roll-ups** (Blackstone/QCIL, Manipal, KKR/HCG) which are clearly DOCUMENTED; (c) **ARPOB escalation**, which is DOCUMENTED across all listed chains and is the dominant value-creation lever in India; and (d) modest, acquisition-driven **debt-loading** at some platforms (HCG gearing 2.08x→3.59x FY24→FY25; Max net cash → net debt). The notorious Fortis/Religare Singh-brothers fund-diversion scandal is an extreme related-party value-extraction case but is fraud, not the standard PE playbook.

**Thread 2 — listed-chain economics.** Indian listed hospitals (Apollo, Max, Fortis, Medanta/Global Health, Narayana, KIMS, Rainbow, HCG) show a consistent structural picture in FY23–FY25: **rising ARPOB (industry +11% FY24, +7% FY25), high-20s/low-30s EBITDA margins, 60–77% occupancy, and a payer mix shifting toward cash + insurance**. ICRA upgraded the sector outlook to "Positive" with OPM guided at 22–24%. These economics — even where PE has fully exited (Max, post-KKR) or never controlled — illustrate the same incentives the PE playbook exploits: pricing power over a captive, insurance-padded patient base, with regulators (insurers, AHPI) flagging the tariff-escalation tension. The listed-chain ARPOB engine is the India-specific value-extraction story; the financial-engineering tools are mostly latent.

## 2. The PE value-extraction playbook (globally evidenced) and India status

For each mechanism: what it is, the global evidence, and **whether it is DOCUMENTED, PARTIAL, or only HYPOTHESIZED in India**.

### 2.1 Dividend recapitalizations
- **Mechanism:** PE owner loads new debt onto a portfolio company to fund a special dividend to itself, extracting cash without selling/improving the company. `VERIFIED FACT` (definitional).
- **Global evidence:** PESP, *Dividend Recapitalizations in Health Care* — describes the tactic as raiding critical infrastructure for short-term profit; e.g., Leonard Green extracted **≥$645M** in debt-funded dividends from safety-net chain Prospect Medical, which filed Chapter 11 in Jan 2025. `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION` (advocacy-research source). https://pestakeholder.org/reports/dividend-recapitalizations-in-health-care-how-private-equity-raids-critical-health-care-infrastructure-for-short-term-profit/
- **Peer-reviewed causal evidence:** NBER Working Paper 33435 (Bhardwaj, Gupta, Howell, Jan 2025): dividend recaps that raise total debt (~84% on average) increase financial-distress probability by ~2.4x the firm mean; increase deal returns but reduce fund returns (moral hazard); reduce employee wages. `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION`. https://www.nber.org/papers/w33435 ; full text https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33435/w33435.pdf
- **India status:** **HYPOTHESIZED / NOT DOCUMENTED at scale.** No verified India hospital dividend-recap found. Indian guardrails: RBI does not police corporate D/E directly, but bank prudential norms, SEBI rules for listed firms, and Companies Act dividend limits constrain the tactic. `EXPERT INTERPRETATION`. https://www.motilaloswal.com/learning-centre/2025/9/dividend-recapitalisation-definition-process-and-example

### 2.2 Sale-leaseback of hospital real estate / hospital REITs
- **Mechanism:** Operator sells land+buildings to a REIT/landlord vehicle and leases them back; raises immediate cash for owners but burdens the operating company with perpetual, escalating rent. `VERIFIED FACT` (definitional).
- **Global evidence:** US studies link hospital REIT/sale-leaseback ownership to higher closure/bankruptcy risk. Harvard T.H. Chan / academic work: hospitals that sold buildings to REITs were **>5x** as likely to close or go bankrupt as comparable hospitals that kept real estate. `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION`. https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/hospitals-acquired-by-real-estate-investment-trusts-associated-with-greater-risk-of-bankruptcy-closure/ ; https://www.statnews.com/2025/12/18/hospitals-acquired-reits-more-likely-close-go-bankrupt/ ; case study: Steward Health Care / Medical Properties Trust (MPT) 2016 sale-leaseback → 2024 collapse. https://commercialobserver.com/2025/05/investment-model-private-equity-sell-hospital-reit-mpt-steward/
- **US scale:** 18 publicly traded US healthcare REITs; MPT ~$14.9B assets; PE poured ~$1T into US healthcare real estate over a decade. `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION` (press-reported aggregate). https://commercialobserver.com/2025/05/investment-model-private-equity-sell-hospital-reit-mpt-steward/
- **India status — KEY FINDING: PARTIAL / mostly ABSENT.**
  - **No listed dedicated healthcare REIT exists in India** as of FY25. A 2017 plan by Toro Finserve for "India's first healthcare real estate fund" ($500M, intent to list in India or Singapore) did not materialize into a listed healthcare REIT. `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION` (absence of any contrary primary evidence). https://ehealth.eletsonline.com/2017/05/toro-finserv-launches-indias-first-healthcare-real-estate-fund-worth-500-mn/
  - **The closest India analogue is the Fortis → Religare/RHT Health Trust structure (see §3.1):** Fortis spun off hospital real estate into a Singapore-listed business trust in 2012 (asset-light), paid recurring service/lease-type fees, then bought the portfolio back in 2019 for an enterprise value of Rs 4,650 crore. This is a real, DOCUMENTED Indian instance of the sale-leaseback economics (operating company paying fees to a real-estate trust), executed via a Singapore vehicle. `VERIFIED FACT` (deal happened) / `EXPERT INTERPRETATION` (that it functions like a sale-leaseback). https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ani/what-s-happening-to-fortis-s-singapore-listed-rht-health-trust-119121200528_1.html
  - Industry commentary notes rent is ~10–12% of topline for Indian healthcare operators where real estate is leased — i.e., the economic lever exists, but a US-style REIT monetization market has not formed. `EXPERT INTERPRETATION`. https://www.colliers.com/en-in/news/healthcare-real-estate-the-need-of-the-hour

### 2.3 Management / monitoring fees
- **Mechanism:** PE sponsor charges the portfolio company ongoing "monitoring/advisory" fees, transferring cash to the GP regardless of performance. `VERIFIED FACT` (definitional).
- **India status — HYPOTHESIZED / NOT DOCUMENTED with figures.** No specific Indian hospital monitoring-fee disclosure was located in this cell. The Fortis–RHT "hospital and medical services agreements" under which Fortis paid service fees to RHT-held entities is the nearest documented recurring-fee arrangement (a real-estate/services fee, related-party, arm's-length per disclosure). `VERIFIED FACT` (fees existed). https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ani/why-is-fortis-buying-the-assets-of-singapore-listed-trust-119012100069_1.html

### 2.4 Debt loading / leverage on platforms
- **Mechanism:** Leveraged buyouts and post-deal debt raise interest burden; combined with recaps, can push operators toward distress. Global causal evidence as in §2.1 (NBER 33435).
- **India status — PARTIAL / DOCUMENTED at deal level, modest at listed-company level.**
  - **HCG (now KKR-controlled):** overall gearing rose from **2.08x (FY24) to 3.59x (FY25)**; interest coverage fell from **3.02x to 2.60x**. `VERIFIED FACT` (CareEdge rating release, Jan 2026). [PDF] https://www.careratings.com/upload/CompanyFiles/PR/202601130131_Healthcare_Global_Enterprises_Limited.pdf
  - **Max Healthcare:** transitioned from net cash to net debt — net debt ~Rs 1,142 crore at Mar-2025 (vs net cash ~Rs 1,041 crore Mar-2024); consolidated borrowings ~Rs 3,010 crore FY25; interest cost ~Rs 60 crore FY24 → ~Rs 165 crore FY25 — but D/E only ~0.32 (acquisition-driven, conservative). `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION` (press/data-aggregator-reported). https://simplywall.st/stocks/in/healthcare/nse-maxhealth/max-healthcare-institute-shares/health ; https://tradebrains.in/max-healthcare-enters-odisha-market-by-acquiring-58-in-kalinga-hospital-for-298-cr-gross-debt-stands-at-3573-cr/
  - **Manipal (Temasek-controlled):** consolidated borrowings ~Rs 10,612.79 crore as of 31-Jan-2026 per DRHP-related reporting; fresh-issue proceeds earmarked ~Rs 5,378 crore for debt repayment. `VERIFIED FACT` (DRHP-reported, press-relayed). https://www.tradingview.com/news/moneycontrol:0e6414fdb094b:0-temasek-and-dr-ranjan-pai-backed-manipal-hospitals-files-for-mega-1-bn-plus-ipo-biggest-ever-in-indian-healthcare/
  - **Interpretation:** Indian listed hospitals are *not* carrying US-LBO-style leverage; debt is mostly capex/M&A-driven, and listed-company balance sheets remain comparatively conservative. The HCG gearing jump is the clearest single signal of post-PE-deal debt loading to watch. `EXPERT INTERPRETATION`.

### 2.5 Buy-and-build add-on roll-ups
- **Mechanism:** Acquire a "platform," bolt on "add-on" hospitals, extract value through consolidation/pricing/operational restructuring.
- **Global evidence:** Kim (SSRN/ResearchGate 2025), *When Buy-and-Build Breaks*: platform hospitals gain persistent profitability; local add-ons see severe volume/profitability contractions; system value driven by operational restructuring. `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION`. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5416094
- **India status — DOCUMENTED and prominent.** This is the dominant *foreign-capital* mechanism in Indian hospitals:
  - **Blackstone/TPG → Quality Care India (QCIL):** Blackstone acquired ~72.5% of QCIL (CARE Hospitals) in 2023; QCIL added KIMSHEALTH (~80%); built an 11-city, ~4,000-bed platform; then 2024 merger with Aster DM to create a top-3 chain (38 hospitals, 10,150+ beds). `VERIFIED FACT` (company releases). https://www.asterdmhealthcare.in/newsroom/detail/aster-and-blackstone-backed-quality-care-have-merged-establish-one-of-top-3-hospital-chains-india ; https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/blackstone-tpg-backed-quality-care-india-to-acquire-kims-for-400-mn-123103000921_1.html
  - **Manipal (Temasek/TPG):** serial bolt-ons (e.g., Khubchandani Hospital, Mumbai, Rs 415 crore, 2024; further Mumbai/Andheri acquisition reported Rs 495 crore, 2026). `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION` (press-reported). https://ehealth.eletsonline.com/2024/10/manipal-hospitals-enters-mumbai-market-with-acquisition-of-khubchandani-hospital-for-%E2%82%B9415-crore/
  - **KKR/HCG:** HCG itself rolls up oncology assets (e.g., majority 51% in Mahatma Gandhi Cancer Hospital). `VERIFIED FACT` (rating release). [PDF] https://www.careratings.com/upload/CompanyFiles/PR/202601130131_Healthcare_Global_Enterprises_Limited.pdf
- **Bain market context:** India was the largest APAC healthcare-PE market by volume in 2024 (~26% of regional deal volume); provider/hospital deal value expected to roughly double from ~$5B (2024) to >$10B (FY2025). `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION` (Bain). https://www.bain.com/insights/india-global-healthcare-private-equity-report-2024/

### 2.6 ARPOB (average revenue per occupied bed) escalation
- **Mechanism:** Raise revenue per bed-day via tariff hikes, specialty/case-mix upgrading, and payer-mix shift toward higher-paying cash/insurance patients.
- **India status — DOCUMENTED across the board (this is THE India value lever).** Industry ARPOB +11% FY24 and +7% FY25 (ICRA); guided 6–8% FY26; driven by speciality/case mix, better payor mix (more cash + insurance), annual price revisions, and tech (robotic surgery). OPM 23.6% FY25; guided 22–24% FY26; occupancy 62–64%. `VERIFIED FACT` (ICRA industry research). https://www.icra.in/Rating/DownloadResearchSummaryReport?id=6458
- **Public-interest tension:** Rising ARPOB is celebrated by equity investors as a "structural tailwind" but flagged as a driver of ~14% medical inflation and rising insurance premiums; AHPI has clashed with insurers over tariff revisions. `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION` (multi-source, partly press). https://thetradable.com/global-economy/indias-hospitals-drive-medical-inflation-as-arpob-climbs-ig--m ; https://www.moneylife.in/article/hospitals-vs-insurers-tariff-battles-put-patients-in-the-crossfire/78365.html

### 2.7 Clinical-quality externalities (global, for context)
- JAMA (Kannan, Song; 26 Dec 2023): PE acquisition associated with **+25.4%** hospital-acquired adverse events (driven by falls and central-line bloodstream infections), across 51 PE-acquired vs 259 control US hospitals (Medicare data). `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION`. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2813379
- ED price markups: PE acquisition raised total charges per inpatient day by ~$407 (7%) and ED charge-to-cost ratios by 16% (2005–2017 study). `STRONG EVIDENCE-BASED CONCLUSION`. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11482842/
- **India status:** No comparable India quality/PE causal study located. `PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS` that similar dynamics could apply; UNVERIFIED for India.

## 3. India-specific value-extraction cases (DOCUMENTED)

### 3.1 Fortis → Religare/RHT Health Trust (Singapore business trust) — the clearest India sale-leaseback analogue
- **2012:** Fortis spun off hospital real estate into **Religare Health Trust (RHT)**, listed on the **Singapore Exchange Main Board** at S$0.90, raising ~**S$510.7 million** — the largest Indian-sponsored business-trust IPO in Singapore. Portfolio: 2 hospitals, 12 clinics, 4 greenfield clinical establishments. Fortis retained ~28% and continued to hold via a wholly-owned overseas subsidiary. This let Fortis go "asset-light." `VERIFIED FACT`. https://www.theasset.com/article/22900/religare-health-raises-s-510-7-million-in-ipo
- **Recurring fees:** Fortis paid hospital and medical services / lease-type fees to RHT-held entities — the operating-company-pays-real-estate-vehicle structure that mirrors a sale-leaseback. `VERIFIED FACT`. https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ani/why-is-fortis-buying-the-assets-of-singapore-listed-trust-119012100069_1.html
- **2018–2019 reversal:** Fortis board approved acquiring RHT's entire India portfolio for an enterprise value of **Rs 4,650 crore** (including ~Rs 1,152 crore of RHT debt); definitive agreements early 2019; **portfolio sold to Fortis 15 Jan 2019** — "full circle" back to owning its real estate. Fortis had an indirect ~29.76% interest in RHT (related-party, stated arm's-length). `VERIFIED FACT`. https://www.business-standard.com/article/companies/fortis-healthcare-looks-to-sell-stake-in-singapore-listed-rht-trust-119041000059_1.html ; https://business.medicaldialogues.in/fortis-completes-acquisition-of-rht-assets-at-enterprise-value-of-rs-4650-crore
- **Interpretation:** This is the single best Indian example showing the sale-leaseback / real-estate-monetization economics actually deployed on Indian hospital assets — and then unwound, suggesting the model's rent burden was judged value-destructive for the operating company. `EXPERT INTERPRETATION`.

### 3.2 Fortis / Religare — Singh-brothers fund diversion (extreme related-party extraction; fraud, not standard PE)
- SEBI/SFIO: funds of Religare Enterprises (REL) diverted via Religare Finvest (RFL) for the benefit of erstwhile promoters (RHC Holding; Malvinder & Shivinder Singh). Scheme diverted ~**Rs 2,473.66 crore** from a material REL subsidiary plus mis-utilisation of ~Rs 487.92 crore of RFL; SEBI ordered loan recall >Rs 2,315 crore. SEBI later fined 52 entities (incl. Fortis Healthcare Holdings) Rs 21 crore total. `VERIFIED FACT` (regulator actions). https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/religare-finvest-fund-diversion-case-sebi-slaps-rs-21-crore-fine-on-52-entities-including-fortis-healthcare-holdings-101652 ; https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/fortis-religare-sfio-malvinder-shivinder-singh-sebi-100938-2018-02-19
- **Classification:** This is promoter fraud / related-party extraction, NOT the foreign-PE playbook. Included as context for how value was siphoned from Fortis before IHH (foreign) took control. `VERIFIED FACT` (events) / `EXPERT INTERPRETATION` (relevance framing).

### 3.3 Apollo Hospitals demerger (2025–2027) — restructuring, NOT a real-estate REIT
- Apollo's "composite scheme" demerges its pharmacy + digital (Apollo 24|7) + Keimed into **Apollo HealthTech Ltd**; CCI cleared 23 Sep 2025; NCLT order 26 Mar 2026; shareholder meeting 24 Jun 2026; targeted listing ~Q4 FY27; new entity targets ~Rs 25,000 crore revenue by FY27. **This is a pharmacy/digital demerger — there is no hospital-real-estate REIT element.** Important to flag because "Apollo + REIT" searches surface this and it must not be mischaracterized. `VERIFIED FACT`. https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/apollo-healthco-eyes-q1fy27-breakeven-amid-group-restructuring-plans-126052101659_1.html

## 9. Gaps / unverified / cautions

1. **No India dividend-recap evidence.** No verified instance of a debt-funded dividend recap at an Indian hospital was located. Treat any such claim as UNPROVEN until a specific filing/loan agreement is produced.
2. **No listed Indian healthcare REIT.** Confirmed absence (as of FY25). The "Apollo REIT" idea conflates Apollo's pharmacy/digital demerger (Apollo HealthTech) with real estate — they are unrelated. The Toro Finserve 2017 fund never became a listed healthcare REIT.
3. **Some ARPOB/margin/occupancy figures are broker/analyst-relayed (STRONG EVIDENCE, press-reported), not pulled directly from each company's filed annual report**, because several primary IR PDFs (Apollo IR deck, Business Standard, law.asia) returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch. Where possible, cross-check against the official IR decks: Apollo (apollohospitals.com IR), Max (max-website20-images.s3...November_17_2025_Investor_Presentation), Fortis (fortishealthcare.com Q4FY25 release), Medanta (medanta.org IR), Narayana (NSE press release linked), KIMS (kimshospitals.com IR deck), Rainbow (cloudfront IR), HCG (CareEdge VERIFIED).
4. **Manipal EV (Rs 40,000 cr) and deal value (Rs 16,500 cr / ~$2bn) are press-reported**, not from a CCI order or company release text obtained here; the TPG release confirms the stake structure (Temasek 41% new, ~59% total; TPG 11%; Manipal 30%) but not the rupee figures. Verify against the Manipal DRHP and CCI order when available.

6. **Payer-mix granularity is thin.** Company decks describe a shift toward cash + insurance and "improving payor mix," but precise cash/insurance/government splits per chain for FY23–FY25 were not all captured; pull from each annual report's MD&A for exact percentages.
